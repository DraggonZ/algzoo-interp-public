# Family mistakes: what decides near ties, and a hand-built network that errs like trained ones (2026-10-18 evening)

Question: other trained 16-neuron networks reproduce about 30% of ARC's mistakes, a generic imprecise solver about 20%, the hand-built networks 5-7% (`../hilton/topdown/README.md`, last sections). What do trained networks share at near ties, and can a hand-built network share it?

- `decide.py`, `cross_split.py` (logs): ARC has no biases, so each score is exactly a weighted sum of the ten numbers on each list. At near ties (third within 5% of second, second positive) the decision is the small leftover of two large compensating parts: the later number's own vote is larger (per unit about 57-66 against 42-47 for the earlier, ARC), and the earlier number discounts it (about −24 to −46 per unit). Own votes range 12-290 per unit across lists; the net advantage of the later number at a tie has median about 0 and spread about ±15 per unit against a vote of about 50. Same in trained networks 111 and 209.
- `residue_predict.py`: the leftover is partly predictable: positions of the three largest plus the largest's size relative to the second predict ARC's choice 86% (always-right baseline 79%; trees, held out); the seven smaller numbers add nothing. Two networks share wrong near ties more than chance (7-9% both wrong against 4.6% independent).
- `shape.py`: trained networks name the third on about 20% of near ties in every arrival order, more when the largest is much bigger (23% under 1.1 times the second, 29% over 3 times). v19 is exact in five orders (0-1%) but names the third 41% in order second-third-largest (a defect of its own).
- `fade.py`: v19 with its clock fading per step (trained range 0.84-0.95; not tuned). rho 0.97: near ties 28% third wins, growing with the largest (18% to 37%), ARC's mistakes reproduced 20.5%, accuracy 90.4%; rho 0.94: 23%, 85.8%. The fade produces the family's kind of leftover (it grows with the hidden amount) but costs accuracy, because the memory's copies of the wave no longer match what is subtracted; the trained network matches its readout to its fading (account item 7), so its leftover must come from elsewhere: partial switching of the cancelling neurons is the candidate.

## Short rules for the cancelling neurons (2026-10-18 evening)

- `canc_sources.py`: the four on their real block and ReLU, keeping only some of their inputs. Dropping the gates' push (zero or average) leaves 80-83% accuracy, 68% of ARC's mistakes reproduced; the memory's push at its average 74%, 40%; input, store, detector and own block only 65%, 39%. The memory and gate inputs are needed.
- `push_roles.py`: on each list the memory's push into each of the four is exactly linear in the numbers; a table of median weights on the largest and the previous largest (by step, steps since the largest arrived, neuron) explains 82-92% of its variance (gates' push 79-85%, except into neuron 1, 3%).
- `canc_rule.py`, `canc_rule2.py`: replacing the memory's or gates' push by those tables costs 10-15 points (mistakes reproduced 63-72%); adding the other numbers by step, position and sign 85-91% accuracy, 67-76%. Still short of the long fitted rule (74%) at far fewer numbers only in part.
- `loop_rule.py`: the four plus the memory as one 12-neuron loop on real weights, with the store, detector and gates pushes into it tabled the same way: 94% (detector), 89% (store), 67% (gates), 64% (all three).
- `compensate.py` (the reason): at near ties (only; on typical lists groups cancel 1.5-2 times, per neuron about 30 times, see ../superposition/README.md, corrections) each group's contribution to the second-minus-third score is large and they cancel: cancelling neurons SD 90 per unit of the second, memory 91 (correlation −0.85), their sum 50, store and detector 46, the whole decision 5. The decision is about a twentieth of its parts, so any rule for one group must be right to a few percent of that group's push on each list; median-weight tables are not. A short explanation has to describe what the groups compute together (the hidden wave and its cancellation, as v18 does by design), with its list-to-list leftover, rather than each group alone. This is also the mechanism behind the near-tie leftover above and behind Hilton's 'dense, not built in levels' remark.

## The leftover as an explanation, and where ARC's mistakes come from (2026-10-18 late evening)

- `leftover.py`: votes per position plus a leftover table on the largest and previous largest by the largest's position and the scored position (210 numbers, least squares on the three largest positions): explains 58% of the variance of ARC's centred scores there; as a stand-alone scorer 84.8% accurate, 23.9% of ARC's mistakes reproduced (the family's level, not ARC's).
- `config_rule.py`: one linear rule in the three largest values per arrangement (36 to 4,320 numbers): 98.5-99.95% accurate, at most 17.4% of ARC's mistakes. Least squares averages the leftover away; it is not a smooth function of the three largest.
- `self_rest.py`: redrawing the seven smaller numbers (below the third) on ARC's grid mistakes, ARC makes the same wrong choice 62% of the time (trained 209 on its own: 65%); still right on 98.4% of the lists it got right. So about a third of a network's specific mistakes depend on the smaller numbers' exact values; 60-65% is about the ceiling for any explanation that uses only the three largest, and the same level as ARC's weight noise and as all blocks replaced by rules (64%).
- `trace_size.py`: at near ties each smaller number moves ARC's decision by a median 0.6% of a vote (90th percentile 6%); all of them together about 0.37 votes RMS (209: 0.16, 111: 1.07).
- `traces.py`: v19 where every number writes eta times a record's wave at its own position into the clock (the erasure mechanism: a record wipes these traces). Its near-tie errors become flat across arrival orders and grow with the largest, like the family's, but too many: eta 0.03 (per-number effect near ARC's 90th percentile) 91.2% accuracy, third wins 31% of near ties, 20% of ARC's mistakes; eta 0.1 82.9%, 38%, 26%. Candidate reason (unverified): v19 hides the traces of numbers since the last record, where the trained network shows them as part of their votes (account item 8).

## Building the family's near-tie behaviour into hand-built networks (2026-10-18 late evening)

- `trace_where.py`: in trained networks the smaller numbers that move near-tie decisions are kept positive numbers, mostly those before the largest (mean |effect| 0.05-0.12 votes per unit at every distance from the second and third, random sign; after the largest 0.02-0.04; negative numbers under 0.01). Per-input traces in v19 had the wrong pattern (negative numbers after the largest acted).
- `crosstalk.py`: ARC's average crosstalk of a kept vote onto other positions is large (neighbours +0.3 to +0.7 of a vote, distant −0.1 to −0.7) and asymmetric. Copied whole into v19's readout (rows mixed) it breaks it (51%; random signs 44%): ARC's own score structure compensates it, v19's does not.
- `leak_match.py`, `leak_v21.py`: the leak scaled by s, with s read off where a kept positive number before the largest moves the decision by ARC's 0.10 votes per unit. v19 cannot carry any leak (s 0.02: 86% accuracy; its correct answers rest on exact tiny margins). v21 (28 neurons, robust) can: s 0.05 93.1% accuracy, third wins 24% of near ties (family 21%), 17.6% of ARC's mistakes; at the matched size (s 0.2) 87.3%, 34%, 23.8%. So trained networks carry per-number effects of about 0.1 votes with fewer near-tie errors than a leak of that size gives; how their per-number effects partly cancel is open. Both v19 and v21 also have a defect of their own in order second, third, largest (third wins about 40%, trained 18%).
