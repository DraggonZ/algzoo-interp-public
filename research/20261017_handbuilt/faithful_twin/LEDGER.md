## The frozen scorer

`score_twin.py` (sha256 `5220e57d569b7dc935a283595a883b7bf9b2225410066e193b46d0dc853dc033`). It imports, and so also freezes: `../superposition/twin/shifts.py` (`305d91ee...`), `../superposition/twin/battery.py` (`26b46872...`), `../family/common.py` (`d345be1f...`), `../hilton/propagate/full_mean.py` (`00e54584...`), `../hilton/propagate/adf.py` (`9ef34add...`).

Four parts: a 12-line jobs checklist (the same test on ARC and on the candidate), the five group transplants into ARC (`../hilton/faithful/faithful.py`), the 12 nudge shifts (`../superposition/twin/shifts.py`), own accuracy on 20 million lists plus near-tie and redraw measures (secondary).

**How the pass bands were set.** From each job's wording and from ARC's own value, so that ARC passes all 12. The first draft had four bands ARC failed; they were widened before any candidate counted: new-largest size correlation 0.9 → 0.75 (ARC 0.82: its store lags a step, so the detector compares with a faded largest); late gate's push 0.2 sd → 0.04 sd and three times the push on 7 and 8 (ARC 0.09 on 1 and 6, at most 0.01 on 7 and 8); hiding reference fit R² 0.8 → 0.6 (ARC 0.66-0.94). The twin's values were visible while setting these, which is a weakness; no band sits between ARC's value and the twin's except job 8 (below).

**Job 8 (hiding rebuilt each step) needed a second measure.** The plain persistence test (`hide_persist.py`: a 10% scale-down of the hiding neurons at step 5, share left a step later; ARC 0.21 of the nudge) gives the matched twin 0.00, because its hiding neurons are mostly a large comparator level that is recomputed every step; the wave they carry on their own is tiny in size but read with readout weights in the hundreds. So job 8 also asks what the readout takes from them, centred over positions (a level shared by every position does not count): ARC keeps 0.73 of the nudge a step later, the twin 0.89; the band is 0.8. This is the narrowest margin in the checklist. Finding on the way: by the decision's view, ARC's hiding neurons also hold most of a nudge (a one-step cut of their own block changes as many answers in ARC as in the twin, 10-75% depending on the step), so "rebuilt every step" is true of their comparator level, much less of what the readout uses.

**Ranking rule (written before round 1).** More jobs passed first. Ties broken by the mean transplant accuracy over the five groups; a gain counts if it is more than 1 point (the transplant grid is deterministic; 1 point is a judgement call covering the grid's own error against sampled accuracy, about 0.1-0.7 points). A candidate under about 90% own accuracy does not count as working. A candidate that adds a job but loses working status does not beat the best.

## Branch r1a

### Plan (written 21:08 UTC, before any scored run)

An analysis read came first (`r1a/a1_slots.py`, `r1a/a1_slots_w0.log`; not the scorer). In the twin each memory neuron is a delay-line cell: memory cell j holds the number that arrived j + 2 steps ago. So the cells that hold the largest at ages 2, 5 and 6 are cells 0, 3 and 4. The cell holding the current largest already sits at about zero (it stores each number's shortfall below the largest) and is off about two-thirds of the time; the readout gets zero from it either way. Separately, cells 3 and 4 already pass the neuron-3 gap test (signs 1.00 and 0.93), through the backward wave a record writes into older cells.

Order of attack:
1. **Job 10 (memory 0, 11, 10 off at ages 2, 5, 6).** Push the largest's own cell below zero by a share eps of its jump d. Route: detector -> linear route weight + eps/a, so the linear route carries + eps d one step after a record; the memory stores minus the linear route, so the cell gets - eps d two steps after. Clipping gives the zero the readout wanted anyway, so the function should barely change. Side effect: a record at step 8 is read at position 8 straight from the linear route and is hidden by kappa - eps instead of kappa. Then relabel: cell 0 -> ARC's 0, cell 3 -> ARC's 11, cell 4 -> ARC's 10. Expect job 10 to pass and accuracy within half a point.
2. **Job 9 (rotating memory).** The delay line has no rotation. Close it into a loop (last cell back into the first with weight w; v23's own option). Its modes then turn by 45, 90 and 135 degrees per step (ARC: 38, 84, 127), with size (w rho^7)^(1/8), at least 0.7 for w >= 0.062. v23's notes say looping costs accuracy (84.7% at w = 0.25), so only a small w can keep 92%. A w picked just to clear the band is a weak form of the job; I will say so.
3. **Job 11 (neuron 3 carries the gap)** conflicts with job 10 in this layout: the gap carriers are cells 3 and 4, the neurons the switching ages need. I will report which the final assignment favours, rather than add a gap term with no role.

Not attempted: the keep gate's cut (needs the runner-up, which needs a 17th neuron or the flip neuron, `../superposition/twin/README.md`), the late gate (ARC's slot 9 holds the twin's linear route, which the twin cannot spare without the nine-positions-in-eight-cells leak), hiding rebuilt each step.

## Branch r1b (round 1; lost on the transplant tie-break; ledger lines kept, code not merged)

### Plan (written 2026-10-21 21:15 UTC, before any candidate run)

Order of attack, cheapest and safest first:

1. **Job 11, neuron 3 carries the gap: pick the right memory slot for ARC's slot 3.** The twin's new-largest neuron writes a backward wave of size K d (d = the new largest's jump over the previous largest) into every memory slot, with a sign set by the slot's offset from the largest. A slot whose wave is negative both one and two steps after the record then rises with the largest and falls with the previous largest, which is the gap. An analysis run (`r1b/gap_by_slot.py`) shows the matched twin's memory neurons now in ARC's slots 11 and 12 pass job 11's test (signs 0.93 and 1.00, gap R² 0.50) while the one in slot 3 fails. Rule: put in slot 3 the memory slot whose backward wave is negative at both ages; the other seven by the old correlation match. Same function, so accuracy unchanged.
2. **Job 4, the keep gate's cut.** The twin's gate passes every positive number (G = beta relu(x)). The network has no second-largest, but a cut at half the largest so far agrees with "x >= 0.75 x the second-largest so far" on 92% of non-record steps (a check on random lists, not on ARC), so G = beta relu(x - 0.5 m1) should pass. A cut makes votes depend on when a number arrived; adding back beta x 0.5 m1(at arrival) linearly through the linear-route neuron (it reads the store) makes each vote alpha x + beta max(x, 0.5 m1), monotone for a fixed arrival. On ideal votes this costs 1.3 points (10 points without the add-back). Risk: the new largest also gets beta 0.5 p (p the previous largest) and may leak through its hiding.
3. **Job 9, rotating memory**, only if 1 and 2 leave room: a weak loop-back (last slot into the first, weight w) turns the delay line into 45°, 90° and 135° rotations; the test needs modulus >= 0.7, so w >= 0.062. Earlier notes measured 11 points lost at w = 0.25, so this may sink accuracy below 90%.

Not attempted: jobs 5 and 6 (late gate) need slot 9, which holds the direct route, and all 16 neurons are in use. Job 10 (switch-offs at fixed ages) would erase the largest's hidden vote from the delay line, which the readout needs. Job 8 needs the hiding wave rebuilt from outside the hiding neurons, and no other neuron holds it.

Keeping it working: every change adds to the twin's rules; accuracy is checked on 1 million lists before each full score.

## Branch r2b (round 2)

### Plan (written 2026-10-21 21:25 UTC, before any scored run)

Start from r1a-3 (`candidates/r1a_keep_corr.py`, 7/12). Of the five failing jobs, four need something the delay-line build lacks: a spare neuron (late gate, jobs 5-6: slot 9 holds the linear route, which the ninth stored position needs), a loop that costs more accuracy than is left (rotation, job 9), or a second holder of the hiding wave (job 8). Job 11 (neuron 3 carries the gap) is blocked only by a layout clash, so it goes first.

1. **Job 11 without losing job 10.** One step after a record the detector writes the hiding wave's backward copy into every memory cell: -K f(k) d into the cell for offset k (sign -1 storage), d = the jump over the previous largest. A cell whose offsets at ages 1 and 2 both have f < 0 carries +d, the gap, in its pre-activation. With the twin's wave (f(0) = 1, f(1) = f(2) = 0) only offsets -3, -4, -5 are negative, so only delay cells 3 and 4 qualify, and they are the age-5 and age-6 switchers. The wave has an 8-step period, so f(-7) = f(1) and f(-6) = f(2): if the forward spill on the next two positions is made slightly negative instead of zero, delay cell 6 (offsets -7 at age 1, -6 at age 2) carries the gap, and it has no switching job. Why a negative forward spill is safe: positions between a largest and the number that overtakes it are below it and can never be the answer afterwards, so lowering them changes no answer; only a positive spill there is harmful. Rule: f(1) = f(2) = -0.05, a round value under the limit 0.065 at which the backward spill f(-2) reaches 1/K (the twin's backward-safety rule). Delay cell 6 goes to ARC's slot 3; the four other free cells to 12-15 by activity correlation. Expect 8/12, accuracy within 0.2 of 91.4.
2. Then, by evidence: look at what limits the transplants (keep gate 67, hiding 16, memory 28), or try a mechanism change toward ARC's references.

**Jobs done ARC's way (orchestrator's addition), my reading of r1a-3 before any run:** store (1) yes, the largest so far; detector (2, 3) yes, beats the largest so far, by the amount; keep gate (4) no, cuts at 0.44 of the largest, ARC at 0.75 of the second-largest; hiding (7) partly, a comparator with the largest only, ARC mixes in the previous largest (the test's fit passes on the largest alone); switch-offs (10) no, a delay cell clipped by a push when it receives the largest, where ARC's are phases of a rotation; blindness (12) yes. So 7 passed as scored, 4 by ARC's mechanism (1, 2, 3, 12).

Design (derived, not yet run). A running second-largest can be carried by **one comparator neuron Q, rebuilt every step**, with no register:
- Q_t = g relu(s_{t-1} - x_t), s_{t-1} the second-largest before x_t.
- Then s_t = m_{t-1} + Q_t / g - relu(m_{t-1} - x_t). Check by cases: a record (Q = 0, clocks 0) gives m_{t-1}; a number between s and m gives x_t; a number below s gives s_{t-1}.
- Every term is in the state at step t: m_{t-1} = store / s; relu(m_{t-1} - x_t) is the clocks' comparator level / g. So Q's row reads the store, itself (+1) and the clocks' level (-1). - Keep gate: K_{t+1} = beta relu(x_t - 3 Q_t / g). If x_t >= s_{t-1} this is beta x_t; if below, beta (4 x_t - 3 s_{t-1}). So it is on exactly when x_t >= 0.75 s_{t-1}, ARC's cut (0.75: ARC's 0.74, rounded; a constant read off ARC). Records are always kept. ### Plan (written 2026-10-21 22:25 UTC, before any run)

Where the neuron comes from: **the flip neuron**. r3b's `a1_noflip` showed that simply dropping the wave's flip part costs 15-20 points, because the remaining 45-degree rotating pair spills too much onto other positions once a largest is overtaken. I think the 45-degree, unit-size rotation is the wrong 2-D wave, not that two dimensions are too few. Derivation (written out in `src/algzoo/handbuilt_v4a.py`):
- With the delay-line memory, the hiding value that a position p gets from a largest at r is g(p - r) = c A^(p-r-1) b, for any 2-D wave dynamics A, start b and copy read c (the readout row for p is c A^(p-9), so copies and subtraction still cancel exactly while the largest stands).
- What an overtaken largest leaves behind (spill) only has to satisfy: g(0) = K (hide by 1.5 x the jump); g(k) <= 0 for forward offsets 1..8 (those positions hold numbers below the overtaken largest and cannot be the answer, so lowering them is harmless; raising them is what hurts); g(-k) < 1 for backward offsets (those positions are at least one jump below).
- A growing rotation A = rho R(theta) gives g(k) = K rho^k cos(theta k + phi) / cos(phi). theta 20 degrees and phi 70 degrees put every forward offset 1..8 at angles 90-230 degrees (g(1) = 0, the rest negative); rho = 4 brings the backward spill to 0.71 x the jump at offset -1 and under 0.24 after. All round values chosen by these rules, not by score. The cost is size: the wave grows to about 4^8 = 65,000 x the jump, so the clocks' comparator gain must rise from 1e4 to 1e10 (float64 keeps about 1e-6 absolute precision there).
The freed neuron becomes the late gate in slot 9: LG(t+1) = d_t (the detector's jump, one step later), on only after records. Its job: the switch-off push into memory cell 0 (r3a's rule, -eps x LG), so ARC's neuron 0 switches off two steps after a record, through the late gate as in ARC.

1. Analysis (not scored): the twin's rules with the 2-D growing wave, no flip, no Q (position 0 cell kept), compared with the same base with the flip wave, on 1M lists. Prediction: within 1 point of the twin with exact level (93.5%).
2. Candidate r4a-1: the full build (2-D wave, Q and keep cut from r3b, late gate in 9). Prediction: MECH 6/12 (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 12); job 6 fails (no push on hiding); score_twin 8/12 (adds 5); accuracy 91.5-93; hiding transplant may fall (wave values far from ARC's scale).
3. If time: the late gate's push on hiding (job 6) or the hiding references (job 7) on the freed geometry.

How I expect it to keep working: the hiding is exact while a largest stands (same identity as the twin), and the spill after overtaking is harmless by construction.

The wave. Per unit of K d at offset k from the largest, f(k) = a cos 45k + b sin 45k + p cos 135k + q sin 135k. The twin's forward-safe rule (f(0) = 1, no spill at offsets 1 and 2) leaves one free coefficient. Every such wave has f(+-4) = -1 and f(+-8) = +1, because both pairs point the same way four steps on. Rule for the free coefficient: no spill at any other offset. That gives a = p = 1/2, b = q = 0, so f is 1 at offsets 0 and +-8, -1 at +-4, and 0 elsewhere. The twin's wave also spilled 0.4-0.6 at offsets -2, -1 and 4-7.

Build rule (`src/algzoo/handbuilt_w7b.py`): every weight of w6b is affine in the wave's coefficients for a fixed turn, and the two turning parts are separate linear systems that share only the comparator level. So w6b is built twice, once with the 45-degree clocks carrying (a, b) and once with the clocks turning 135 degrees and carrying (p, q), both without a flip part. The no-wave network comes from linearity, checked on both builds. Weights among the other neurons are A + B - (no wave). Each pair's clocks keep their own rows. Every reader takes each pair's wave with that build's weights (only the part that sums to zero over the three clocks, since the level is read from C7). So the readout's hiding, the linear route's forward copy, the backward writes into memory, the margin deliveries and the H7 relay all follow the twin's rules for the new wave.

Start: `candidates/r6a_gap.py` (MECH2 9/12, strict 6, 95.2%). Direction set by the orchestrator: move the hiding wave out of the hiding comparators, so that slots 1, 6, 7 hold comparators rebuilt each step and the wave is held elsewhere. Horizon about 5 experiments, done by 01:50 UTC.

Worked out on paper first:
- **What holds the wave must itself be a comparator.** The wave has to be wiped when a record arrives. A ReLU network without biases can only wipe a signed 2-D state by clipping it, so the wave must ride on a positive offset that drops to zero at a record: the comparator level gc (m - x). That is why the clocks exist. Any "elsewhere" is more neurons of the same kind (level + two wave directions).
- **A design that makes slots 1, 6, 7 rebuilt each step: a two-set relay.** Set C (the present clocks) and set R (three relays) are the same kind of neuron, but each reads the wave only from the other: C(t+1) = relu(gc (m_t - x_{t+1}) + A z(R_t)), R(t+1) = relu(gc (m_t - x_{t+1}) + A z(C_t)). The record's start b d is written into R only, so the wave lives in R at steps L+1, L+3, ... and in C at L+2, L+4, ...; the other set holds the level alone. Every reader of the wave (linear route copy, readout, ring compensation) reads z(C) + z(R). The function is unchanged exactly. R goes into slots 1, 6, 7; C goes elsewhere. Then a nudge to R at step 5 is gone at step 6 (R is rebuilt from C), which is job 8's test; silencing R one step after the largest deletes the wave that hides it, which is the version-2 hiding test.
- **The count does not fit in 16 neurons.** The relay costs three neurons. Two if R has two neurons and takes the level from C (R_i = level + f_i . z, decoded with C's level). r7a's trick frees one (the linear route). So the relay is one neuron short at best, two if neuron 3's gap keeps its own neuron. I see no other neuron that can go without losing a scored job: store, detector, keep, late gate and runner-up each carry a passed job, and the seven delay cells each carry one position (dropping one loses about a tenth of the lists).

Experiments:

Prediction: own accuracy equal to r6a_gap (95.2) to rounding; with R + runner-up as the hiding slots, job 8's activity share about 0.1 (only the runner-up keeps a nudge) and readout view well under 0.8; hiding test (silence R and the runner-up one step after the largest) about 50-80%; silencing R alone higher. 2. (analysis, 18 neurons) the same with two relays that take the level from C. Prediction: the same numbers.
3. If both hold: try to reach 16 by r7a's keep-gate-first (frees the linear route) plus one more neuron. I do not have a second neuron yet; if none is found, no 16-neuron candidate is scored and the result is the measured shortfall.
