r6b summary. Best rule: `rules/overwrite_store.py`, **NET +117.9**, CHAIN 0.0000 PASS, a gain of **+8.0** over `delay_kick.py` (+109.9). Mechanism: the detector compares the arriving number with the value the store is about to take (its recurrent row is −c × the store's), and a record overwrites the store (λ = a c, so c = W[2,2] / W[2,4] = 0.560, no constant). Together the store/detector block is λ times a nilpotent block. What I learned about the accounting: a relation with residual 0.025 on weights of size 0.04-1.7 saves only about 19 of the 42 bits that row 4's recurrent weights cost, because these weights need far finer precision; one fitted constant plus its budget share costs about 22 bits, so the same relation scores −19 alone (r6b-1) and +8 when a mechanism fixes the constant and the budget share is already paid by the base rule. Failures: the detector's drive is not a linear read of the largest and second-largest (R² 0.88; the age of the largest matters), so row 4 cannot be solved from states; no second stated vector cleans the detector's residual; zero-constant statements on the store row (no input weight, blindness) are exact but cover two weights and stay at the floor. The gates (rows 5 and 9, ceilings 69 and 73 bits) were not attempted beyond looks: every relation I found for them (keep gate reads only hiding 7 and 8, its memory read along the detector's write, cosine 0.79) needs a fitted constant per row and would lose by the same arithmetic as r6b-1.

The gates' rows carry little curvature (late gate: every weight under one curvature unit), so they can gain a few bits at most. Mechanisms to test, cheapest first:
1. The detector compares the arriving number with the store as it will be one step later. The store's next value is its row applied to the state (0.97 s + 1.73 d + small reads), so the detector's recurrent row is −β × the store's row. With row 2 uncovered (stated anyway), row 4's 16 recurrent weights follow from one constant β. The raw weights agree in sign on most columns.
2. Exact running maximum: a record x puts a·(x − threshold) into the detector and the store adds g times that. For the detector to compare against the fresh store, β·g = 1, so the detector's self-weight is −1 with no constant (ARC −0.99), and β = 1/g comes from the store's uncovered weight 4→2: a rule with no constant.
3. Gates: the keep gate reads the detector and the hiding neurons through hinges; look for ties of row 5 to stated rows only if 1-2 work and the curvature makes it worth it.
