## Goal and success criteria

Primary: advance or stress-test the explanation of ARC's trained network. Secondary: advance or stress-test interpretability methods. Stretch: ARC's challenge, a mechanistic accuracy estimate that matches random sampling's error for the same compute. A result counts when it is confirmed by intervention or on held-out lists or networks, and is stated with what it does not show.

## Prior work (`lit_review/README.md`)

Nobody has fully explained M16,10 or met the challenge (Hilton, 17 August 2026). Chen's 2024 notes, Goldowsky-Dill's repository, Keane's fork (about 50 notes) and Mariappan's preprint each have parts of the account; the paper and article credit them. Earlier hand-built networks need 21 and 22 neurons. "Fully understood" check (`lit_review/fully_understood.md`, quotes verified at ARC's post): ARC fully understands only its 2- and 4-neuron networks of this family (10 and 32 parameters), each with a same-size hand-built network; do not claim ours is the largest fully understood network, or rare in having a same-size rebuild. The shift-register test is partly new (Karuvally et al. 2023; Keane's note 49); no size-matched, role-by-role rebuild was found (`lit_review/shift_register_and_rebuilds.md`).

## The account of ARC's network (current)

1. Neurons 2 and 4 keep the largest so far (neuron 4's self-weight −0.99, as in ARC's hand design).
2. One step after a number arrives, neuron 5 keeps it if it is at least about 0.75 of the second-largest so far (0.6–0.85 across lists; lower when the second arrived late, because neurons 7 and 8 store a late second weakly), including every new largest; other numbers are forgotten after one step. Confirmed by forcing neuron 5 off and on.
3. A kept new largest starts a timer: twelve neurons turn about 40° per step, mostly in one plane, read by the output like a dial with spokes about 43° apart. Its pattern on the scores is a broad wave (neighbours at about 0.7 of the peak), and neurons 1, 6, 7 and 8 cancel it within a few units at every position (`votes/cancel_shape.log`).
4. A bigger number drives 1, 6, 7, 8 to zero, which unhides the old largest's vote. On random lists: silencing them after the largest arrives makes its position win in 70% (random four 5%); restoring them leaves the network right in 16% (random four 92%) (`methods/silencing_random_lists.log`, `artificial_wipe.py`).
5. Votes slide one position per step and compete. In its decisions the network behaves like fair votes, equal per unit across positions within about 10%, with a discount by the runner-up only at positions 8 and 9 (`votes/vote_relative_fit.log`). At the level of scores, votes fade with arrival time and later votes are discounted by about 0.7 of the runner-up through switching in neurons 3, 11, 12 and 15; the two roughly offset (`votes/vote_profiles.log`, `vote_interaction.log`).
6. Mistakes are close calls decided by small vote-size errors. They are network-specific (median correlation 0.02 across 22 networks; 0.5% weight noise reshuffles them), equally common for neighbouring and distant pairs, and mostly within 1–3% gaps. Random per-position size errors of about ±3% in v3 reproduce this pattern roughly (`votes/v3_gain_noise.log`), which supports small random biases in general, not vote-size errors specifically. Mistake kinds (`votes/mistake_kinds.log`): 61% name the third (55% within 3%, 29% at 3–10%), 15% name the largest (90% within 3%), 18% have at most one positive number, 6% name the fourth or lower (mostly gaps over 10%); about two-thirds are close calls within 10%.

7. **The timer is a fading shift register, read through matched weights** (`timer/timer_shift.log`, `timer/timer_spokes.log`). In the most common on/off pattern at steps 4–8, ARC's dynamics have rotating pairs at ±43°, ±83°, ±125° and 180° (near the 40°, 80°, 120°, 160° of a nine-slot cyclic shift), shrinking to 0.84–0.95 per step. The output weights for position k+1 equal those for position k pushed one step through the dynamics (cosine 0.97; plain neighbouring rows 0.56). Consequence: a vote at any position reads the same height at its own position, and every vote's profile has the same shape around its position, so votes are fair in decisions even though raw activities fade. Most other networks show the same relation (cosine 0.8–0.98 in 17 of 21; 0.46–0.64 in zoo2, s103, s107, s112). v4's memory is the exact, non-fading version of this object. Caveat: one on/off pattern per network (12% of steps for ARC); switching is not covered.

8. **Overlapping jobs and the erasure (2026-10-18, `gao/README.md`).** Neuron 2 also carries when the largest arrived (a slow wave, about a sixth of its value), passed mainly to the cancellers and the memory. Neuron 8 carries where the second-largest sits, read out directly (part of its vote). The cancelling neurons hold a small share of the votes cast since the current largest arrived (about three times more per unit than earlier ones), so a new largest, by silencing them, wipes that share. For the number right after a record this share is decisive: putting it back after the overtaking makes the network name that number on 51% of such lists (2% normally; random same-size share 14%; `gao/unerase.log`). Across 22 trained networks, erasure sits wherever that network's revealed wave spills (r+1 in 4, r+2 in 6; rank correlation −0.68, −0.93).
9. **Memory switching.** Without it, 44%. Neuron 0 switches off exactly two steps after every record, keeping the gates' push (about −4.3 x the new largest) out of it (that rule alone: 93.6%); neuron 15's switching follows the timer's phase, no simple rule.

Partly explained: neuron 3 helps positions 1, 5 and 6; position 8 is read through neuron 5 and position 9 through the last input's dip. Every trained network has neurons a new largest silences (85% cutoff); 4 of 21 pass both wipe tests (`artificial_wipe_models_v2.log`).

10. **Corrections from steering tests (2026-10-18, `superposition/steer/README.md`).** Small nudges with predicted sizes corrected items 3-5: the memory holds each earlier vote next to a larger negative imprint of the largest, and the store holds the largest and discounts later arrivals by it; after a few steps the largest's vote is cancelled jointly by hiding neurons, memory and store; a kept vote enters directly and through the detector one step later, about half each, and a last-step number only directly, which sets the late-number bias (removable by a 1% turn-down of the last input, at a small cost in overall accuracy).

## Hand-built networks

Details: `paper/draft_v3.md` Section 4 and Appendix A; run history in README.
- v5 (16 neurons, 98.97%, `algzoo.handbuilt.build_v5`): exact, own algorithm (keeps every positive number, non-fading memory, trim by a dip); shows the parts suffice in 16 neurons.
- v7 (role copy, 88%): fails when a record arrives at the last step (11%); sharp-switch fixes needed 17-18 neurons (not a lower bound).
- v18, the twin (16 neurons, 96.0% on 1,000,000 fresh lists; `algzoo.handbuilt_v18`): trained layout, jump-driven clock, delay-line memory with exact level, second-largest store reassigned as a flag, late gate reassigned as a flip neuron; lacks the erasure (83% vs trained 96% on the neighbour lists). A 2-D clock erasure never beat the base (`v17/v18_erase_oracle.log`).

Detail (earlier text, kept):

Summary (2026-10-17): v5 is exact in 16 neurons with a different algorithm from ARC's. With ARC's roles, every exact (sharp-switch) design we found needs 17 or 18 neurons. ARC's own 16-neuron solution is a set of approximate tricks, each measured below. Details of every attempt are in the run history ([README.md](README.md)).

- **v2, 16 neurons, 98.9%** (`algzoo.handbuilt.build_v2`): running maximum, second-largest with gates, last input, three rotating registers; one lasting vote. Accuracy follows from the design (99.02% predicted and measured).
- **v4 and v5, 16 neurons, 98.9% and 98.97%** (`build_v4`, `build_v5`; tests in `tests/test_handbuilt.py`). Every positive number that is not a new largest leaves a sharp vote in a 9-neuron shifting memory. The current largest sits there as a broad wave hidden by a 3-neuron clock. A bigger number wipes the clock (the wave shows); one step later the memory dips by a share of the old largest, trimming its wave to a spike of its value and clearing older votes. v5 replaces v4's clip neuron by a fourth cancelling neuron z = g((1 + 0.001) m1 − x), so its input weights have ARC's layout (one large positive, four large negative). `build_v5(clock="ringup")` (98.92%) rebuilds the clock from the largest so far instead of injecting the new largest's value. Differences from ARC: v5 keeps every positive number (no second-largest store, no forgetting); its fourth canceller is z, where ARC's neuron 8 holds the second-largest (intervention: setting neuron 8 moves neuron 5's keep threshold about 45% of the way; `cancellers/n8_intervene.log`); its memory has nine slots and no fading.
- **v7, ARC's roles neuron by neuron, 88%** (`scripts/handbuilt_v7.py`): store and detector (ARC 2, 4), keep gate relative to the runner-up (5), late new-largest gate (9; on in 97% of steps right after a new largest and 1% otherwise, `cancellers/n9_gate.log`), 3-neuron clock plus second-largest store (1, 6, 7, 8), 8-neuron memory (0, 3, 10–15), positions 8 and 9 read outside it. 98% on ordinary lists. Its two clocks fail on different edge cases (`cancellers/v7_split.log`, `v7_endfix.log`, `v7_hybrid.log`): a clock rebuilt from the largest so far carries an offset, so a new largest at step 9 leaves the store's correction as a phantom (11%); a clock fed by the late gate keeps a stale wave after back-to-back new largests (50%). A readout from the late gate fixes step 8 (11% to 99%), and ARC's neuron 9 readout does the same job (removing it drops ARC from 97% to 93% on those lists).
- **Exact needs 17–18 neurons.** v7 plus z (17 neurons) reaches 96%; adding a ninth dial angle (18) reaches 98% with all edge cases at 98–100%. z supplies the new largest's value on the next step only when it is a new largest; the ninth angle stops step 8 sharing position 0's angle. Without z, forgetting relative to the largest instead of the runner-up fails (ordinary lists 90% to 53%; `cancellers/v8_m1forget.log`), and keeping every number biases positions 8 and 9 through the memory's mean removal (`v8_split.log`). So ARC's roles need both z and the second-largest store for exactness, and ARC has room for one.
- **Graded attempts at 16 neurons, all below v7** (`cancellers/v9_split.log`, `v10_try.log`, `v11_mix.log`, `v12_broad.log`, `v12_pushdown.log`): a jump-driven clock hides correctly but its reveal is broad (68%); a dip after the new wave enters also clips the new wave (v10, 11–63%); a value-wave memory with a jump-driven clock leaves a wave of the old largest at the current one, which wins because v7's waves are five times a vote (39–52%); a larger broad reveal trades revealed against kept lists (89% against 76%); a push-down done in the readout lets the old largest's own spill push it down (1–13%). Lesson: these tricks only work together, at vote-sized waves, in a memory that pushes down at write time.

## How ARC does it in 16 neurons (measured)

Table 2b (`cancellers/table2b_exclusive.log`): v7 fails only at the last step; ARC 94% there. Measured mechanisms (draft v3 Section 4.4): smooth comparisons (cancellers half off near 0.9 of the largest), a clock driven by the jump (fits), a memory split at overtaking (interventions on twin runs), and the erasure (account item 8). Demoted (averages or fits only): runner-up-relative scores, mutual push-down, one-sided reveal, leaky hiding, non-fading modes.

Detail (earlier text, kept):

Table with kinds of list that do not overlap (`cancellers/table2b_exclusive.log`): the role-copying network (v7) fails only on a new largest at the last step (11%; 97–99% elsewhere); +z (17) 89% there; +ninth angle (18) 100%. ARC 94%. "17–18 neurons" describes our sharp-switch designs, not a lower bound.

1. **Smooth comparison instead of sharp switches.** Cancellers switch off gradually from about 0.9 of the largest (`arc_canceller_thresholds.log`); at the last step detector and cancellers decide together (an interchange test (check not included in this repository)); with a rival at 0.97 of the old largest, ARC errs 17% when the new largest is 1% above the old one, 4% at 30% above. Evidence: counts and an interchange.
2. **A clock driven mostly by the jump.** Each canceller fits gain × (switch-off point − input) + timer wave (R² 0.94–0.996; switch-off points blend the largest and second-largest; 7 has no timer, 8 stores the second-largest) (`arc_canceller_code.log`); after a new largest their push is about new − 0.7 × old, the same after back-to-back new largests (`arc_b2b_stale.log`). Evidence: fits and a matched comparison.
3. **The memory splits its work when the largest is overtaken** (`v13/overtake.log`, twin runs). Two steps later, memory 10–13 change with the jump (new − old), while 0, 14, 15 are pushed down by the old largest's value only. Copying either group from the twin run lowers naming the old largest from 98% to 60–87%; zeroing the late gate's −1.42 weight onto neuron 0 lowers revealed-old-largest lists from 96% to 90%, kept-number lists unchanged at 94% (check not included in this repository). Evidence: interventions. This is the split the role-copying network lacked: it dipped the whole memory, which also clipped a jump-driven clock's new wave.

Demoted (averages or fits only): runner-up-relative scores (fit, about two-thirds of variance; conflicts with the decision-level fit that finds no discount at positions 1–7); mutual push-down (depends on the zero point of the scores; fair margins also appear in the hand-built network, so they come from the task); one-sided reveal (the next position often gets about half the peak); leaky hiding (+8 is a mean; median 0); non-fading memory modes (need neurons 0, 3, 15 on; Section 3.3's modal pattern fades). Averaged slopes by role predict ARC's answers only 60% of the time. Closed: the "four cancellers must carry five quantities" framing; the near-tie margin fit (collinear by construction); the memory as a delay line of canceller dips.

## Accuracy estimate (stretch goal)

Exact close-call integration along 720 lines per network (`bias_formula_v4.py`, `pl_flip.py`): root-mean-square miss 0.65 points on 22 development networks, 1.02 on 16 later ones. Not a challenge result: sampling at the same cost misses by about 0.1 points; the method treats the lines as a sample, so it is only partly mechanistic (`estimate_split_v4.log`).

## Where the reading gap comes from (closed)

A nonlinear reader recovers about 40% of ARC's mistakes, but similar shares of the hand-built network's, so this is mostly generic to soft switching (`readout_vs_memory_hb.log`; details in `paper/draft.md`).

## Open hypotheses

- H3 (answered 2026-10-17): older votes are pushed down by later ones, not cleared. A later kept number lowers every earlier position by about a third of its own push; a new largest much more (ARC tricks 2 and 3).
- H4 (answered, account item 7): the timer and its fading follow from the weights under the usual on/off pattern; 17 of 21 other networks show the same relation.
- H6 (v13 premise, untested): ARC's tricks work only together: vote-sized waves, no trim, push-down at write time in a memory that only reaches positions already written, and a mostly jump-driven clock whose hiding is shared with the memory's own modes and the store's readout.

## Gao's substitution tests (2026-10-17/18, `gao/README.md`)

Summarized in the post (`paper/lesswrong_post.md` Sections 3-4) and draft v3 Section 7. Key results: a simulation score measures exactness, not whether the idea is right: on the twin a misreading scores 59-69%, the right idea with one gain 2% off 58%, timer 0.5° off 76%, and adding noise does not separate them, only exact rules pass (`gao/twin_graded_near.log`, `twin_graded_curve.log`); trained formulas 51%; tests scored on the answer cannot see forgetting, score-level tests can (trained 0.02-0.03, twin 1, forgetting hand-built v7 0.000, `gao/twin_forget.log`); role claims fall short (91.3%), resampling features beat random ones when fed back (87% vs 74-83%), the cancellers' precision curve stalls at 91%; cross-network results in account item 8.

Detail (earlier text, kept):

Simulation (replace activations with values computed from the account's formulas, scored by downstream KL): every trained neuron fails alone, even at 99.8% of variance matched; all 16 leave 51%. The cause is precision: the cancellers need about 0.1-0.2 units, the account misses by 0.6-4. v18 passes from its written rules (96.0%, KL 0) and fails just as badly (48%) at our account's level of detail. Switches only: gates right, cancellers cost 6 points, memory 17. Weak activations (below 1% of typical) matter about equally in trained and hand-built networks.

Resampling (input-feature scrubbing; replace a group's activations with those from a list that agrees on the claimed features): calibrated on v18 (true claims pass exactly, a wrong one gives 87%). Redrawing numbers two or more steps old below about 0.75 x the second-largest leaves the trained network at 94.9% (vs 95.3%), but v18, which keeps them, passes equally (check not included in this repository): such numbers can never be the answer, so this tests output invariance, not forgetting. Role-by-role claims fall short (91.3%): neuron 2 also carries when the largest arrived (a slow wave, about a sixth of its value), neuron 8 where the second-largest arrived, the cancellers the previous record, the number after the current largest and other kept numbers. Supported: each trained neuron depends on more features than its role names; not shown: superposition. Simulation coefficients fitted to downstream KL: 51% to 65%. Done (`gao/sim_rich.log`): with the scrub-found features and KL-fitted coefficients, simulated cancellers leave 87% (account's features 64%, 20 uninformed features 76%); no formula of this size passes.

- Memory switching (2026-10-18, `gao/linearize.log`): the memory works almost as a linear register; only neurons 0 (19 points) and 15 (7 points) need to switch off, for all kinds of list; six linear plus 0 and 15 switching gives 89.5%. Neuron 0: off exactly two steps after every new largest, which keeps the gates' push (about −4.3 x the new largest) out of it; that rule alone gives 93.6% (`gao/n0_rule.log`). Neuron 15 open.

## Bridge to the trained network (2026-10-18, `bridge/README.md`)

Next aim (2026-10-18): keep working on bringing the hand-built network closer to the trained one, at 16 neurons. Relabelling and rescaling neurons are exact symmetries (same function), so they are allowed; no weight is fitted. At the trained network's gain scale (gains 10, 92.1%), the matched v18 has nearly the trained input weights (cosine 0.99), the same largest-so-far 2x2 block and the same drive into the hiding neurons; its clock and flip track trained 7, 6, 8 and 1 at r = 0.90-1.00. Still different: the memory (a delay line that writes each record's hiding wave slot by slot and stops when the record is overtaken; the trained memory recirculates, eigenvalues 0°, ±38°, ±84°, ±127°, 180° at 0.82-1.02) and the late gate (v18 uses that slot as a flag). Making v18's memory recirculate costs 28 points, all on lists with overtakings, because the overtaken record's wave then stays at later positions; in the trained network that tail is small in its sum over positions 0-8 but not position by position (single later positions up to +19 per unit, position 9 −17 to −44; `bridge/tail_cancel2.log`), by a route not yet identified. Ideal vote model: eight memory dimensions cannot hold sharp votes for nine positions; the trained memory's uneven angles keep nearby votes sharp (|K| <= 0.1 out to offset 6) and push the error to offsets 7-9 (`bridge/ideal_angles.log`; interpretation). On the same lists, six of the eight trained memory neurons are linear functions of v18's memory and store at 86-97% of variance (plain input features 70-83%, v5 82-91%; `bridge/memory_as_slots2.log`), but random rotating memories fed v18's writes do nearly as well (81-96%; `bridge/memory_control.log`): the networks write the same things, and the test does not show how the trained network stores them. The exceptions are neurons 3 and 15. Not the tail mechanism: memory switching (`bridge/tail_switch.log`) or the stopped feed from the hiding neurons (`bridge/feed_keep.log`). Neurons 3 and 15 are small differences of large, well-matched terms (pre-activation 4-5% of its terms' summed size; `bridge/n3_cancel.log`), so they need not hold anything v18 lacks. Only one v18 gain needs to be large: the clock level (g 10 to 100 lifts the trained-scale build from 92.1% to 95.5%; `bridge/g_sweep.log`). Correction: the loss is not partial switching as such. Records that leave the hiding group partly on are as common in the trained network (6.3% of records); zeroing the leftover lifts v18 to 95.7% and the trained network only to 95.7% from 95.3%, because on those lists v18's median margin is 0.1 against 9.8. v18's near-tie (it hides only the jump) is what makes a small leftover decisive. The trained network partly switches on 78% of lists without loss. Feeding its memory the hiding neurons' unclipped values at partial switches costs 7.5 points (`bridge/clip_consistency2.log`), but the same change with random signs costs as much across 16 networks (`bridge/clip_consistency_across2.log`), and undoing the clipping consistently costs 24 points (`gao/partial_function.log`); so partial clipping does work of its own, and a first reading that it only needs to be seen consistently was retracted. Found (2026-10-18, `bridge/n6_n8_threshold.log`, `clip_which2.log`): one step after a record the hiding neurons' pre-activations are exactly linear in the previous largest p, the new largest and the arriving number x; neurons 7 and 1 hold about 13 (new largest − x), neurons 6 and 8 about 11-13 p − 11-12 x, because they get no jump from the detector and the store lags one step. So 6 and 8 compare the number right after a record with the second-largest, and that is the partial switch one step after a record; it falls on neurons that do not yet carry the record's wave, so the hiding loses nothing (the group's sensitivity to the largest turns by under 2°, against 19-26° in v18 at small gains, `clip_recovery.log`). This ties together the resampling finding that the cancellers carry the previous record and the number after the current largest, and the erasure through neuron 8. The same pattern appears in 4-10 of the 17 other trained networks with hiding neurons, depending on the cutoff (`bridge/second_comparator_cutoffs.log`). Next: build a hand-built hiding group that injects the jump into two neurons and lets the rotation carry it to the other two.

## Neuron rules toward Hilton's estimate (2026-10-18, `memory/README.md`, `hiding/README.md`, `gates/` in progress)

Two agents worked out per-group rules; their headline numbers were checked against their logs. All rules are analysis fits on the trained network (allowed), not hand-built weights.

- Memory (0, 3, 10-15): exactly linear while none switches, turning at five speeds (a slowly growing level, pairs at 38, 84, 127 degrees per step, a flip). Without switching 44%. Neuron 0 is off exactly two steps after every new largest; 10, 11, 12, 15 switch off where the current largest's wave crosses zero in them (ages 3-6). The linear machine switched by fitted sign rules gives 93.6% on fresh lists (network 95.5%), but the rules hold about 8,300 fitted numbers; a short on/off table by step and age gives 87% (`memory/sign_rules3_fresh.log`, `rule_size.log`).
- Cancelling neurons (1, 6, 7, 8): input about 10.6-13.2 × (reference − new number), the reference a mix of the previous and current largest fixed by age; a clipped neuron's excess passes to the others next step in weight-fixed ratios, so the references track a running second-largest. Substituted rule: 94.4% against 95.4% on fresh lists, near-tie overtakings 84% against 89%; 648 fitted numbers (`hiding/rule_ladder.log`, `subst_ladder.log`).
- Late gate (neuron 9) makes the margin at overtakings: zeroed one step after an overtaking, near-tie overtakings fall from 94% to 8% right (size-matched random control 88%); removing its push on the four cancelling neurons gives 0.1%. Removing its push on the memory raises near-tie overtakings to 99% (on that subset only; whole-list effect not checked) (`hiding/margin_intervene.log`). Gates (`gates/README.md`, `g28_final_rules.log`, each gate replaced at every step, two fresh sets of 200,000 lists): the late gate is off unless a new largest arrives, then about 1.5 × it; a 7-number rule costs 0.63-0.66 points, tables by the two largests' ages 0.02-0.04. Its push one step later on cancelling neurons 1 and 6 keeps the new largest hidden; without it lists whose answer is an overtaken record fall from 95.5% to 49% (`g12_n9_job.log`). The keep gate's on/off decision is 'a record, or at least c × the second-largest', c 0.65-0.8, 95.3% against 95.4%; its value must be exact (5% error costs 0.7-0.9 points) and the passing value rule (−0.64) has about 550 fitted numbers. Both gates replaced: −0.69 with tables, −1.2 to −1.3 with the late gate's short rule. Surprise: forcing the keep gate off below the exact second-largest raises accuracy from 95.4% to 95.9% (new111: 96.1% to 96.5%), cuts at 0.8-0.95 change nothing (`g25_forget_at_second.log`, `g26_forget_more_new111.log`); the network does not have the exact second-largest, so this is a bound, not a fix.
- Visual explainer of Hilton's propagation, with real numbers from the trained network: `viz/propagation.html` (built by `viz/propagation/data.py` and `build.py`). It shows the largest so far against its exact formula, the store against the largest by age, the leftovers of a clean and a fade rule (the clean rule's leftover drifts to about −2.8 × the largest at age 7; with the fade it is centred but spreads to about ±2.5), and why scores must be tracked jointly (right beats the main rival on 98.7% of lists, 78.9% if paired at random; the nine separate chances multiply to 92.9% against 95.4%).

- Sampling-free estimate, top three exact and the other seven at their truncated mean (`hilton/README.md` last section, `hilton/propagate/`): ARC 95.32% against 95.319% true (20M lists); 33 trained networks RMSE 0.52 points (run 3's estimate 0.94 on the newer 16); 40 copies with ARC's weight noise RMSE 0.51, correlation 0.991. No fitting, no sampled lists, but far more compute than sampling (10M fixed-input runs miss by 0.2). A Gaussian model of all 16 neurons step by step failed (39%): it blurs the sharp switches.
- Hilton's metric for the hand-built network itself (`hilton/README.md`, `hilton/faithful/`): with v18 as the idealized weights and the gap as noise, predicted accuracy is 10% (chance) against 95.3%; patching hand-built values into the trained network one group at a time leaves 85% (store and detector), 75% (late gate), 49% (keep gate), 26% (memory), 16-19% (cancelling neurons). Faithful for the store-detector pair only; the rest reaches the same answers another way. The idealized weights for Hilton's test must be a cleaned-up copy of the trained network.
- Cheap sampling-free estimate (`hilton/cheap/README.md`, agent, verified against `final.log`): exact tie-band widths by linear solves along each gap, 720 tie configurations, a fixed scaling rule and a density table; about 1,750 runs per network; RMSE 0.57 on ARC + seeds 200-215 (development), 0.82 on seeds 100-115 (held out, scored once), against sampling 0.63 at the same runs. Counted in RNN steps (shared prefixes) it is about 850 step-equivalents, where sampling gives 0.9. The floor is the reduction's own 0.52-point error (sampling reaches it at about 2,600 lists); a closer reduction is the open item. Note: sampling's per-list spread over the 33 networks is 26.5 points (21 for ARC's alone).

## Ideas backlog

- A robust twin (design principle from `gao/switch_vs_cancel.log`, `twin_level_sweep.log`): low levels, so less cancellation, with rules written to work through clipping, as the trained network's cancellers do with partial switching.
