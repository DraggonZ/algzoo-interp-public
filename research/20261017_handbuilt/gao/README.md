# Gao's tests on the trained network and on the hand-built v18

Leo Gao proposes judging an explanation by substitution (LessWrong, "Neural networks are not already clean", 2026-10-17, and "An ambitious vision for interpretability", 2025-12-05; both fetched and read): replace a model's activations with values simulated from the explanation and measure the downstream KL from the real output. A good explanation leaves the output unchanged. This folder applies that test to ARC's 16-neuron network (95.35% accurate) and, as a calibration, to our hand-built v18 (96.0%), whose every weight follows from a written rule. All scores use 200,000 random lists (seed 202); fitted coefficients come from another 200,000 (seed 101). Shared code: `simlib.py`.

## Result in one paragraph

**Corrected (see the corrections section below).** Two tests. Simulation (replace activations with values computed from the account's formulas) fails for the trained network; the hand-built one passes from its exact rules. Resampling (replace activations with ones from a list that agrees on what a claim says matters) shows that the output does not depend on numbers two or more steps old below about three-quarters of the second-largest (94.9% against 95.3%). But v18, which keeps those numbers, passes equally (96.1%), so this does not test forgetting: such numbers can never be the answer. Resampling also shows the store, keep gate and cancellers depend on more features of the list than their roles name.

### Simulation in detail

Our account of the trained network fails the simulation test, and the calibration shows the failure is real, not a quirk of the test. Simulating any single trained neuron from the account's formula costs accuracy, even when the formula matches 99.8% of the neuron's variance. Simulating all 16 leaves 51%. The cause is precision, not pattern: the network needs the four cancelling neurons accurate to about 0.1-0.2 units and the others to about 0.5, and the account misses by 0.6-4 units. The hand-built network passes the same test exactly when simulated from its written rules (all 16 neurons: 96.0%, no measurable KL). But it fails just as badly when described at the level of detail of our trained-network account (48%). What the account does get right is the switching of the gates: letting the account decide only which neurons are on, with values from the network's own weights, leaves the store, detector and both gates unchanged, costs 6 points through the cancellers and 17 through the memory, whose switching the account does not describe.

## Resampling test of the account's claims (causal scrubbing)

Gao's second test, circuit faithfulness (Chan et al. 2022, causal scrubbing). A claim says which features of the list a neuron group depends on at a step. To test it, the group's value at each step is taken from the trained network run on a second list that agrees with the real one on those features and is redrawn elsewhere (each redrawn number comes from the normal distribution below a bound, so nothing the claim keeps changes). If the claim holds, the output does not move. Control: keep the same count of numbers at random places. Code `scrub.py`; 100,000 lists.

**Positive control on v18** (`scrub_v18.py`, `scrub_prevrec.py`, logs alike). Claims true by v18's rules pass exactly: store and detector, keep, memory (every positive number kept), clock with the previous record (all KL 0.000, 96.0%). A claim known to be wrong fails: the clock without the previous record's value, 87%. Random controls: 8-39%.

**First claims on the trained network** (`scrub.py`, `scrub.log`). Random controls: 14-35%. Late gate 94%, memory 91%, keep gate 83%, store and detector 77%, cancellers 74%, all at once 70%.

**Debugger loop** (`scrub_refine.py`, `scrub_neurons.py`, `scrub_canc_more.py`, `scrub_cur.py`, `store_age.py`, logs alike; chosen on seed 202). Adding one feature at a time to each claim showed what the roles leave out:
- Store neuron 2 also carries when the largest arrived and the second-largest (76% to 94% when kept). Directly: after the 16.6 x largest so far is removed, a slow wave over the largest's age remains, up to about 17% of the value (`store_age.log`). Neuron 4 is nearly clean.
- Is neuron 2's timing signal the erasure the hand-built network lacks? No (`n2_age_where.py`, `.log`): with only neuron 2 resampled and the largest moved, 17% of answers move, evenly across kinds of list (overtaken second 15%, with the third right after it 13%, all lists 17%; rerun 2026-10-18 after fixing a bug in the resampling code that could overwrite the largest with the second-largest when both were kept but the largest was moved, which had given 36%). Neuron 2 acts as one more timer neuron in general use.
- Neuron 8 carries where the second-largest arrived as well as its value (78% to 94%); neurons 1 and 7 a little.
- The keep gate needs when the largest arrived (83% to 91%).
- The cancellers need the previous record (as v18's clock does), the number right after the current largest (the vote the trained network erases), and beyond those, the kept numbers: under the memory's claim they reach 95.2%, more than under any role claim (93.3%).
- The memory's claim as first written redrew the current input; read as the account states it (a number is forgotten one step after it arrives), it nearly passes (91% to 94.9%). Its keep threshold barely matters (0.5-0.85 all within a point).

**Refined claims on fresh lists** (`scrub_final.py`, `.log`, seed 303; trained 95.3%). The single claim "once a step has passed, a number the keep gate forgot (below about 0.75 x the second-largest before it) affects no neuron" redraws about half of each list (4.75 of 10 numbers at the last step) and leaves the network at 94.9% with 97.5% of answers unchanged (KL 0.03). The same count kept at random places: 22-30% (`scrub_power.log`). Role-by-role claims together: 91.3% (KL 0.35); the shortfall is mostly the keep gate (91.4% alone) and the cancellers (93.2%).

## Does the memory's switching matter? (`linearize.py`, `n0_where.py`, logs alike)

Removing the ReLU from a group (its neurons can go negative and never switch off): memory 44%, keep gate 55%, store, cancellers and late gate near chance. Per memory neuron: 12 and 13 change nothing (95.3%); 3, 10, 11, 14 cost under a point each; neuron 15 costs 7 points; neuron 0 costs 19. Keeping switching only in neurons 0 and 15, with the other six linear: 89.5%. Neuron 0's switching matters for lists whose second is an overtaken record (96% to 80%) and for lists whose second came after the largest (95% to 74%) alike, so it is not the trimming after an overtaking.

Neuron 0's switching is one timed event (`n0_off.py`, `n0_rule.py`, `n0_age2.py`, logs alike). After a positive number has arrived, it is off in 100% of steps exactly two steps after a new largest, and almost never otherwise (1% at one step, 0% at three to seven steps; 16% at the new largest's own step). At that step its pre-activation is about −4.3 x the new largest (+1.9 x the second, +0.8 x the previous record; variance matched 93%; mean −4.0): the keep gate and late gate fire one step after a new largest, and their push lands on neuron 0 one step later. Switching neuron 0 by the rule "off exactly two steps after a new largest" instead of its ReLU gives 93.6% (no switching 76.5%, real 95.4%). So the ReLU keeps the gates' push, which carries the new largest's value, out of neuron 0 while the other memory neurons receive it. Neuron 15's switching is less regular (off 47% at a new largest, 31-44% two to five steps after, 0% from six on). A linear probe on the role-free quantities predicts its off state on 80% of steps 3-9 (always-on guess 71%), with the largest's timer waves (180, 160 and 40 degrees) as the main inputs (`n15_rule.log`): it behaves like a timer neuron cut off at some phases. No simple rule found.

## An internal test of forgetting that does discriminate (`forget_internal.py`, `.log`)

The output-based resampling test of forgetting passed on v18 too, so it could not tell forgetting from storing. Measured inside instead: nudge one positive number by 0.01 and see how far the memory neurons' final activity moves per unit. Numbers the account calls forgotten (below 0.75 x the second-largest before them) against kept numbers (not the list's top two), same position, 200,000 lists (seed 303). Trained network: forgotten numbers move the memory 33-42 times less than kept ones (ratio 0.024-0.030 at positions 2-7; none can be forgotten at position 1). v18: ratio exactly 1.000 at every position (each positive number is written at 0.25 per unit). So this test separates a forgetting network from a storing one, and the trained network forgets.

## Why the twin fails fitted formulas (2026-10-18; `twin_gains.py`, `twin_rightvars.py`, `span_check.py`, `fit_on.py`, `fit_relu_out.py`, `fit_active.py`, `fit_active_v18.py`, logs alike)

- Not its gains: rebuilt with clock, flag and detector gains from 10,000 down to 10, the twin still drops to 74-75% with all 16 neurons replaced by role-free fitted formulas.
- Not only missing variables: adding its own clock variables (the jump as a 45-degree wave, the alternating flip term) to the menu does not help under least squares (71%), although its exact clock formula lies in that menu's span (residual 1e-10).
- The fit target: least squares on pre-activations is dominated by deeply-off steps whose pre-activation does not follow the formula. Fitting on on-steps only is worse (the formula cannot learn where to switch off); fitting to post-ReLU values by gradient descent was unstable. An active-set ReLU regression (refit on steps that are on or predicted on, target = activation) lifts the twin's clock and flip neurons to 92-95% each with its own variables. Its memory neurons stay at 79-89% even with every positive number by age in the menu: each slot holds a history-dependent mix (which records were overtaken and when) that the menu cannot express. On the trained network the active-set fit changes little (all 16: 61%).
- So failing simulation with fitted formulas reflects the formulas' variables and fitting, not whether the mechanism is right; only exact rules pass.
- Repeated KL fits (`sim_rich_seeds.log`, three seeds, 6,000 steps): cancellers 66% (account's 5 quantities), 77% (20 blind), 87% (20 with resampling features), identical to three decimals across seeds.

## Undoing the erasure (`unerase.py`, `.log`)

Lists where the second-largest is an overtaken record at r and the third sits at r+1 (19,810 of 400,000). Just before the overtaking, the cancelling neurons' share of the r+1 number's vote (real run minus a run with −0.5 at r+1) is measured; after the overtaking silences them, the share is put back. Accuracy falls from 97.0% to 47.4%, and the network names the third on 51.4% of these lists (2.3% normally). A share of the same size in a random direction: 61.8% accuracy, third named on 14.1%. Same procedure for a third two places after the record: 98.1% to 83.4%, third named 8.4%. So the erasure specifically stops the number right after an overtaken record from winning.

In other networks (`unerase_across.py`, `.log`; cancelling neurons by the 85% rule): new111, which erases r+1, behaves like ARC (third named 3.2% to 40.2%, random same-size share 13.9%; accuracy 95.8% to 58.5%). new102 and new106, which erase r+2, do not: putting back their cancelling neurons' share of the r+2 number's vote lowers accuracy (83.3% to 60.1%, 96.9% to 53.1%) but the third is named less often, not more (13.5% to 4.3%, 2.4% to 0.8%; random 19.4%, 16.2%). So their erasure does not run through a share held by the cancelling neurons. Their cancelling neurons do hold a share of that number's effect that the overtaking wipes (`r2_holders.log`: new102 neurons 3, 5, 6; new106 neurons 1, 2, 7, 8; ARC 1, 6, 8), but putting back the share from just before the overtaking does not revive the vote. Splitting the number's final own-score response by neuron (`r2_route.py`, `.log`; q = 5) shows the erased vote sits mostly in one cancelling neuron in all three networks: ARC neuron 8 (42 when the record stays, 0 when overtaken), new106 neuron 2 (25 to 1), new102 neuron 6 (18 to 1). So the shared mechanism holds (the neighbour's vote is carried by a cancelling neuron, which a new largest silences); the simple restore intervention reproduces it only in the r+1 erasers, probably because in the r+2 erasers the vote builds up in that neuron after the step we restore. A final-state version (add the share the cancelling neurons would hold if the record had not been overtaken; `unerase_final.py`, `.log`) changes little even in ARC (third named 2.3% to 4.4%, random 5.9%), so it fails its positive control and is not a valid test. Summary: the restore intervention shows the mechanism in the r+1 erasers (ARC, new111); in the r+2 erasers the per-neuron split points to the same mechanism, unconfirmed by intervention. new111 at r+2, where it does not erase: third named 2.0% to 15.4% (random 8.3%).

## Which kept numbers the cancelling neurons hold (`canc_kept.py`, `.log`)

Nudging a kept number (not the list's top two) by 0.01, the four cancelling neurons' final activity moves 2.3-2.6 per unit if the number came after the list's largest, 0.8-0.9 if before (positions 3-6, 400,000 lists); the memory neurons move 15-24 either way. So the cancelling neurons hold a small share of the votes cast since the current largest arrived, and a new largest, which silences them, wipes that share: the general form of the erasure.

One role-free set of 30 formula inputs for every neuron (input, largest and second-largest, jump and its lag, the largest's wave at five angles, the record's jump as a wave, previous record, the number after the current largest, kept numbers by age), least squares, 50,000 lists. ## Corrections

- The forgetting claim passes on v18 too (check not included in this repository): it tests that the output ignores these numbers, not internal forgetting. Downstream KL cannot test claims that never reach the output.
- The account-style v18 simulation omitted v18's own quantities (the jump d, the alternating term); with them, clock and flip reach about 80% (`jump_v18.log`). So the 48% mixes a wrong account with v18's fragility (gains of 10,000); it does not show that a right account must fail.
- Fitting the simulation coefficients to downstream KL instead of least squares lifts all 16 from 51% to 65%, cancellers 56% to 64% (`klfit_*.log`, not converged).
- "Precision, not pattern" is half right: errors harm by size, but the scrubbing found structured missing variables, so the formulas omit small structured parts.
- "Overlapping jobs" and superposition overreach: a rotation spread over neurons makes each neuron depend on several features without superposition. Say "depends on more features than its role names". Scrubbing here resamples inputs, so it is input-feature scrubbing, not a test of paths.
- In "all 16" simulation and "all at once" scrubbing, the last step overwrites every neuron, so the output depends only on the substituted final state.

Calibration hypothesis for partial switching (`partial_calib.py`, `.log`): rejected. If partial switching only scaled a kept number's vote down to an overtaken record's, undoing it should hurt most where the third arrived 1-3 steps after the largest and make the network name the third. Instead those lists lose least among the large groups (95% to 80%, naming the third barely changes), and lists whose third came before the largest lose most (96% to 64%). New wrong answers are scattered: a non-positive number 19-43%, the position one past the second 4-43% (the spill of Section 2), the third 12-59%. Undoing the clipping corrupts the cancellers' state broadly; it is not one function.

Why about half a degree (`direction_tolerance.py`, `.log`): at the readout, turning the four cancellers' final state by θ changes the lead by at most θ·|d_C|·|h_C|; the four's push in the worst plane is a median 31 times the lead, so the worst-case tolerance is a median 1.9° (10% of lists below 0.4°, 28% below 1°). The measured costs (turning at every step in a random direction: 0.5° loses 2.5 points, 1° 8.5, 5° 48) are a few times smaller than the share of lists below each worst-case tolerance (13%, 28%, 76%), as expected for random rather than worst directions; compounding works the other way. Order-of-magnitude agreement only.

Tie offsets and cancellation across 22 networks (`tie_sensitivity_across.py`, `.log`; offsets from 720 placements of the top three, 3 copies at 0.5% weight noise). Networks that cancel more have larger tie offsets (rank −0.61 with lead/pushes) that move more under 0.5% weight noise (−0.77; relative to the offset's own size only −0.42, p 0.05). Larger offsets go with lower accuracy (−0.89), as they must, since an offset is a band of close calls the network gets wrong. Reading (interpretation, correlational): cancellation amplifies small weight details into tie offsets, and the offsets cost accuracy; this would explain why accuracy tracks lead/pushes (0.75, `robust_across.log`). ARC's network: offsets 0.016, change 0.021 at 0.5% noise (`scripts/tie_bias_stability.log`).

First-order (attribution-patching) prediction of error costs (`recurrent_condition.py`, `first_order_subst.py`, `weighted_fit.py`, logs). Backward pass through the actual run with on/off pattern fixed: g_9 = w_o[winner] − w_o[runner-up], g_{t−1} = W_hh^T (mask_t g_t). Predicted lead change of an error e: Σ_t g_{t,n} e_{t,n}. Whole-network recurrent condition number: median 220 (readout 46).
