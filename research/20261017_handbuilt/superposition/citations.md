# Citations for the superposition line (checked 2026-10-18 at arXiv abstract or proceedings pages)

- Distributed alignment search: A. Geiger, Z. Wu, C. Potts, T. Icard, N. Goodman, "Finding Alignments Between Interpretable Causal Variables and Distributed Neural Representations", arXiv:2303.02536 (2023); Proceedings of the Third Conference on Causal Learning and Reasoning, PMLR 236 (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536 , https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/geiger24a.html . DAS learns rotations so that interchange interventions on subspaces match a high-level causal model.
- Transcoders: J. Dunefsky, P. Chlenski, N. Nanda, "Transcoders Find Interpretable LLM Feature Circuits", arXiv:2406.11944 (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11944 . A transcoder approximates a dense MLP layer with a wider, sparsely activating one.
- TopK sparse autoencoders: L. Gao et al., "Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders", arXiv:2406.04093 (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093 . k-sparse autoencoders with a TopK activation.
- Linearization of trained RNNs: D. Sussillo, O. Barak, "Opening the Black Box: Low-dimensional dynamics in high-dimensional recurrent neural networks", Neural Computation 25(3):626-649 (2013), doi:10.1162/NECO_a_00409. Fixed points and linearized dynamics around them.
- N. Maheswaranathan, A. Williams, M. Golub, S. Ganguli, D. Sussillo, "Reverse engineering recurrent networks for sentiment classification reveals line attractor dynamics", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.10720.
- Switching linear dynamical systems: S. Linderman et al., "Recurrent switching linear dynamical systems", arXiv:1610.08466; conference version "Bayesian Learning and Inference in Recurrent Switching Linear Dynamical Systems", AISTATS 2017, PMLR 54:914-922.
Only the abstracts or proceedings pages were checked, not the full texts; quote nothing beyond these summaries without opening the papers.
- Compact proofs: J. Gross, R. Agrawal, T. Kwa, E. Ong, C. H. Yip, A. Gibson, S. Noubir, L. Chan, "Compact Proofs of Model Performance via Mechanistic Interpretability", arXiv:2406.11779, NeurIPS 2024. Proofs of accuracy lower bounds for 151 small Max-of-K transformers; shorter proofs need more mechanistic understanding; 'compounding structureless noise' is named as the key obstacle to compact proofs (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11779
- Parameter decomposition: L. Bushnaq, D. Braun, L. Sharkey, "Stochastic Parameter Decomposition", arXiv:2506.20790 (2025); successor of attribution-based parameter decomposition (APD, original listing not checked). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20790
- ARC's AlgZoo post: "AlgZoo: uninterpreted models with fewer than 1,500 parameters", Alignment Forum, https://alignmentforum.org/posts/x8BbjZqooS4LFXS8Z ; per the search summary: an explanation of a model optimized for a loss is a mechanistic estimate of that loss, judged by mean squared error against compute, compared with sampling. (Earlier quotes from this post were verified in lit_review/.) ARC's White-Box Estimation Challenge 2026 (predict a network's expected per-neuron activations from its weights better than by sampling) was found in the same search; its page was not opened.
- Sloppy models: M. Transtrum, J. Sethna et al., review in J. Chem. Phys. 143, 010901 (2015, from the file name; full text not read); R. Gutenkunst et al., "Universally sloppy parameter sensitivities in systems biology models"; Waterfall et al., PRL 2006. Sensitivity eigenvalues spread over many orders of magnitude.
- Superposition: N. Elhage et al., "Toy Models of Superposition", arXiv:2209.10652 (2022).
- Compact proofs, full text read (arXiv v14 PDF, 2026-10-19), quotes: "compounding structureless error terms as a key challenge for generating compact proofs on model behavior" (Section 1); "When we don't know how noise composes across model components, establishing a bound requires pessimizing over the ways the composition could occur. Worst-case noise can quickly grow over components even when the empirical noise is small" (Section 1); "future work can address the problem of compounding structureless errors, perhaps by relaxing from worst-case pessimal ablations to typical-case heuristic guarantees" (Conclusion); limitations: "one-layer attention-only transformers on a toy algorithmic task". Relation to our work: our error model (typical behaviour per arrangement plus the leftover treated as noise drawn from other lists, `errmodel/`) and the stiff/sloppy measurements are a typical-case treatment of structureless error, in a recurrent network, which is the direction they leave as future work.
