# Level 2: predicting accuracy under weight noise from explanation quantities (2026-10-18)

Status: done for this round. The network's named structural quantities do not carry the noise response. Three decision-level quantities, read from each noisy copy on fixed lists, carry most of it.

## Question

ARC's 16-neuron network names the position of the second-largest of ten random numbers, right on 95.3% of lists. Adding N(0, s²) noise to every weight lowers accuracy, and copies differ a lot: at s = 0.0025, ARC's example size, copies range from about 75% to 94%. Hilton's second question asks whether the explanation predicts each copy's accuracy from a handful of quantities it names, computed from that copy's weights. The bars to compare against are the first-order gradient prediction (0.25 points of error per copy, `../../../hilton/hilton_noise.log`) and the ten most sensitive weight directions (0.68 points, `../../../hilton/hilton_lowrank.log`). Both propagate the noise through 50,000-100,000 lists' exact gradients.

## Results

**The structural quantities miss what sets the cancellation's precision.** These are what the account names in the weights: the hiding block's rotation angle and shrink per step, the comparator gains, the store's ratio and leak, the detector's threshold, each comparator's reference from the store, the memory's rotation angles and decays, and the late gate's weights (35 numbers, `quantities.py`). Even the best possible use of them reaches a correlation of only 0.52 with the true accuracy. That best use keeps only the part of each copy's noise they can see and propagates it exactly to first order (`span_greedy.log`). Single quantities reach correlation 0.35 at most (`screen.log`). Noise moves them by tiny amounts, for example 0.17 degrees in the hiding rotation, and accuracy hardly depends on those moves.

**What carries the response is how far each neuron's drive is off, per unit of the largest, at a given number of steps after the largest arrived.** For the weights into the hiding neurons, the sensitivity sits two to six steps after the largest, late in the list. It does not sit at the moments a comparator switches: steps whose number is within 10% of the largest account for only 1-2% of it (`directions.log`). So noise hurts by changing how much each neuron holds while the largest is hidden. That breaks the cancellation of the largest's vote. It does not hurt by moving switch points. With 13 neurons (hiding, memory, store) in 4 age bins, these "levels" reach a correlation of 0.98 under exact propagation. A greedy selection needs 10 of them for 0.93 and 16 for 0.96. The sensitive directions are more economical: 5 give 0.98 (`span_test.log`, `span_greedy.log`). These levels are fixed weightings of the copy's rows, read off the clean network's states.

**Three decision-level quantities predict accuracy within about one point at ARC's noise size.** Each copy is run on 720 fixed canonical lists, one for each arrangement of the three largest. The values are 1.2, 1.0 and 0.85, and the other seven numbers are set to −0.46. On each list we take how much the runner-up's margin changes, relative to the clean network's margin. The three quantities are the RMS of that change over the largest, when the runner-up came before it and when it came after, and over the third. The link is accuracy = 0.1 + (clean − 0.1) · exp(−Σ k_c q_c²), with three fitted numbers. Its quadratic start reflects that a trained network loses accuracy quadratically at first. It saturates at chance. Fitted on 200 copies at s = 0.0025, it was tested unchanged on the 100 held-out shared copies at each noise size (`probe_v2.log`; truth from 2M lists, `../truth/`):

| noise s | per-copy RMSE (points) | correlation | constant guess | 720 random lists sampled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.001 | 0.22 | 0.93 | 0.57 | 0.94 |
| 0.0025 (fit size; cross-validated) | 0.93 | 0.96 | 3.26 | 1.14 |
| 0.0025 (held out) | 0.83 | 0.96 | 2.76 | 1.14 |
| 0.005 | 4.4 | 0.94 | 7.5 | 1.65 |
| 0.01 | 14.7 | 0.91 | 10.4 | 1.95 |

The ranking of copies holds at every noise size, with correlation 0.91-0.96. The level drifts at larger noise, where the model underpredicts. The same link with absolute margin changes predicts mean accuracy 78% against a true 80% at s = 0.005, and 50% against 62% at s = 0.01 (`probe_link.log`). Compared with the earlier bars, this is worse than the gradient prediction (0.25) and the ten sensitive directions (0.68). It uses far fewer numbers: 3 quantities, 3 fitted coefficients and 720 canonical runs per copy. The gradient prediction needs about 100,000 lists × 9 leads × 432 gradients. At the same cost as plain sampling (720 random lists), the model is better at s ≤ 0.0025 and worse beyond (`sampling_control.log`).

**Mistake kinds (bonus).** As noise grows, more of the mistakes name the largest. A straight-line fit to the two largest's-leak quantities predicts each copy's share of lists answered with the largest at correlation 0.80 (s = 0.001) and 0.76 (s = 0.0025). It underpredicts the rise at larger noise: it gives 5.6% where the truth is 8.2% at s = 0.005, and 9.7% against 14.3% at s = 0.01 (`probe_link.log`).

**Transfer to three other trained networks (seeds 106, 111, 115; 150 copies each at s = 0.0025; `transfer.log`).** The quantities need no role labels, because they are margins on the same canonical lists. Refitted on each network, they predict at correlation 0.85, 0.93 and 0.89 (RMSE 2.1, 1.6 and 1.3 points; constant guess 3.1, 4.1 and 2.6). With ARC's coefficients unchanged, the copies still rank well (correlation 0.86, 0.92, 0.87), but the level is biased low by 2-8 points. In network 106 the error reaches 10 points. So the kind of quantity carries over, but the weights on the three quantities do not. In 106 the leak with the runner-up after the largest varies twice as much as in ARC and matters little. I did not compute the structural quantities on the other networks, because they already fail on ARC.

## Answer to the question

Which explanation quantities carry the noise response? Not the structural constants: rotation angles, decays, gains, ratios and the late gate's weights. They reach correlation 0.52 at best, so the account in that form misses what sets the cancellation's precision. What carries it is the size of the leftovers that are supposed to cancel. These are the hidden largest's leak against the runner-up, and the third's margin. With them the account captures the response: correlation 0.96 at s = 0.0025 with 3 numbers, and 0.91-0.96 across noise sizes. A judgement call that may be wrong: these three are read by running the copy on 720 designed lists. They are named by the explanation ("hide the largest down to the runner-up's vote"), but are not computed from its formulas. The explanation says what to measure, not how the weights set it. The bridge between the two is the per-neuron levels by age, which take 10-16 numbers to reach 0.93-0.96.

## Method notes

- Copies: `make_copies.py` regenerates `hilton_noise.py`'s 200 copies exactly (accuracy on its 100,000 lists matches to 0). It also draws fresh copies at s = 0.001 and 0.005 and for networks 106, 111 and 115. Truth comes from 1M shared lists (`copies.npz`). Held-out evaluation uses the coordinator's shared copies, `../truth/noisy_truth.npz` (100 per size, 2M lists).
- "Exact propagation" (`leads.py`, `span_test.py`) keeps the part of each copy's noise inside the span of the quantities' gradients. It then moves 50,000 lists' nine leads to first order, as `hilton_lowrank.py` does. It is an upper bound on what a set of linear quantities could deliver, not a small model.
- The screen's quadratic models on structural plus level quantities (`screen.log`) reach correlation 0.89 cross-validated with 8 quantities (45 fitted numbers). They fail when moved to another noise size (RMSE 1.6 at s = 0.001, against 0.56 for a constant guess), so they are overfit in form.
- Scripts: `noiselib.py`, `quantities.py`, `make_copies.py`, `screen.py`, `directions.py`, `leads.py`, `span_test.py`, `span_greedy.py`, `probe_margins.py`, `probe_link.py`, `probe_v2.py`, `transfer.py`, `sampling_control.py`; each has a `.log`.
