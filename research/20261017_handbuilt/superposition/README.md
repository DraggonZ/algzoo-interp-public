# Superposition and polysemanticity in ARC's 16-neuron network, via a wider hand-built network (started 2026-10-18 late evening)

One-page synthesis: [SYNTHESIS.md](SYNTHESIS.md).

Idea (2026-10-18): the trained 16-neuron network is dense; a wider hand-built network with one clear role per neuron that behaves like it is a designed analogue of a sparse dictionary (as in sparse autoencoders), and can be used to measure the trained neurons' polysemanticity and superposition, and as the idealized model for Hilton's accuracy test. The post is parked for this.

## First tests (v21, 28 neurons, as the candidate dictionary)

- `role_map.py`, `role_map2.py`: each trained neuron's activity as one linear map (shared over steps) of a candidate's neurons plus the current number, held out. v21: 94% of variance on average (store and detector 1.00; neuron 3 0.66); v18 87%; random 28-neuron networks 48-55%; plain features (numbers seen, running largest and second-largest) 54%. Greedy selection: 1-6 v21 roles per trained neuron for 90% of the fit (store, detector 1; most 3-6); random networks 1-18. But ARC's readout applied to the mapped activity gives 55% accuracy (random 21-26%): the 6% unexplained is decisive, as expected from the twentyfold compensation (`../family/compensate.log`), and as with sparse autoencoders whose reconstructions raise the downstream loss.
- `reverse_map.py`: v21's roles read from ARC's 16 neurons: mean 85% of variance (store, detector, flag 1.00; clock 0.93-0.95; memory slots 0.75-0.97; v21's extra hiding neurons 0.57-0.84); from random 16-neuron networks 37-58%, plain features 44%. Effective dimension (participation ratio): ARC 2.9, v21 3.8; v21's 28 neurons keep 99.6% of their variance in 12 directions and 99.9% in 16. Canonical correlations between the two states: five above 0.96, then 0.89 falling to 0.43.
- Reading: v21's extra width buys clean on/off gating, not extra linear dimensions; its roles span about 12-16 directions, as ARC's neurons do. So 'more roles than neurons' here is not superposition in the usual sense (more sparse features than dimensions). The roles that could be superposed are the rarely active ones (flags, gates firing at records, reveal events), packed onto shared neurons by switching. Next: measure which v21 roles are sparse and whether ARC carries them with interference.
- Caveat: rotating pairs (clock, memory) have no preferred basis, so counts of roles per neuron partly reflect how v21 is drawn; subspaces, not neurons, are the fair unit.

## ARC as a switching linear system (`patterns/`, 2026-10-18 night)

With no biases and ReLUs, each step is exactly linear once the on/off pattern is fixed, so the network is a switching linear system; the method is to count its patterns, find each neuron's switching rule, and test which switches matter, before rebuilding.
- `patterns.py`: the memory uses 104 on/off patterns (top 30 cover 98.6% of steps), the hiding group 16 (top 3 90%), the gates 3, store and detector 4, all 16 neurons 1,189. The memory's patterns are combinations of single neurons switching off at set times.
- `switch_rules.py`: per-neuron rules (held-out accuracy of small trees). Hiding neurons are comparators: 7 off when the number exceeds the largest (99%), 1 and 6 above about 0.85-0.87 of the largest (94-98%), 8 above about 1.05 of the second-largest (96%). Memory: 0 off exactly two steps after the largest arrived (96%), 11 off at five steps, 10 at six (98%), 13 and 14 off in early steps when the largest so far is tiny (95-98%). Switching reshapes the memory's rotations (all on: 38°, 84°, 127°, 180° and a slowly growing level at 0.82-1.03; with 15 off: 61°, 115°).
- `content_rules.py`, `content_rules2.py`: neurons 3, 12, 15 have no simple list rule (linear threshold on the largest, previous largest, second-largest by age: 81%, 96%, 81%; adding past numbers by lag 90%, 97%, 86%; boosted trees 96-99%): they switch on the memory's own contents, so their description is 'a ReLU on its reading of the memory'.
- `which_switches.py` (ARC with memory neurons linear, rule-switched or real): all linear 46% accurate; neuron 0 by rule 81%; 0, 10, 11 by rule 87%; + 15 real 91.6%; + 3 real 92.5%; all but 0, 10, 11 real 92.9% (85% of ARC's mistakes). With 0, 10, 11 and 15 real and the other four linear: 93.4%, 82% of ARC's mistakes. Reading: the memory is a linear eight-dimensional rotating system in which four neurons clip (0, 10, 11 at fixed ages, 15 on content); the age rules cost about 2-3 points against the real clips (they miss edge cases), the four rarely clipping neurons about 2.

## Rebuilding the memory cleanly: the precision wall (`rebuild/memory_clean.py`, logs)

Base: ARC with memory neurons 0, 10, 11, 15 real and 3, 12, 13, 14 linear (93.4%, 82% of ARC's mistakes).
- Rounding the memory's eigenvalues, directions kept: angles to 1° 93.1% / 81%; to 5° 89.8% / 62%; sizes to 0.01 93.0% / 76%; to 0.05 90.8% / 59%; both coarse 87.5% / 53%. The five angles and five sizes must stay as measured, to about 1° and 0.01.
- Readout built by the matched-readout rule (row k+1 = row k pushed one step through the dynamics), anchored at row 0 or 4: rows match ARC's at cosine 0.77-0.97 for positions 0-7, but accuracy falls to 41-51% (ARC's mistakes 26-34%). The rule is approximately true and far from precise enough. An earlier version pushed in the wrong direction (cosines near 0); corrected.
- Reading: because the decision is about a twentieth of its parts, any rule standing in for ARC's numbers must match them to about 1%. A clean description of ARC can name the structure (rotating modes, clip directions, comparators) and keep ARC's measured values, but simplified values break it.

- `shuffle_typical.py`: ARC's typical behaviour (one linear rule in the three largest values per exact arrangement, fitted on other lists) is 98.5% right among the three largest; ARC's leftover from it (RMS 5.7 against 27.6 for the decision quantities) borrowed within arrangement and ratio bin gives 94.1% (ARC 95.7% on the same measure), reproduces 29-30% of ARC's wrong choices (family 26%), near ties third wins 15% (ARC 21%). Borrowed from any list: 90.7%, 23%. So the clean network must reproduce ARC's typical behaviour per arrangement, not only solve the task; v7z solves it but its typical behaviour differs from ARC's.

## Error model with hand-built clean parts (`errmodel/shuffle_top3.py`, `gap_parts.py`, `gap_structure.py`, `gap_by_order.py`, `oracle_halves.py`)

- Whole score vectors are the wrong unit (hand-built networks put huge arbitrary scores at positions that cannot win; `shuffle_gap_v21.log`); decision quantities among the three largest are used instead.
- Clean part + ARC's gap borrowed within arrangement and ratio bin: v21 84.8%, v19 kappa 0.5 84.5%, v7z 85.3% right among the three largest (ARC 95.7%). Each gap is as large as ARC's decision quantity (0.7-2.0 against 1.0 votes).
- v19's gap is 86% systematic per arrangement (92% with ratio bins; v21 59%, v7z 70%); what remains is about ARC's own leftover size. **Decision (autonomous): v19 kappa 0.5 (16 neurons) is the clean base, replacing v21.**
- Structure of v19's systematic gap: arrival order alone 52%, the three positions additively 51%, order crossed with each position additively 75%, full arrangement table 86%.
- Per order (`gap_by_order.log`): ARC's largest-minus-second score hardly depends on the second (coefficient −0.29 to +0.24) and falls about 0.3-0.6 votes per unit of the largest, in every arrival order, also when the second arrived after the largest; v19 does this only when the second is an overtaken record (otherwise −1). Reading: ARC hides the largest only down to the runner-up's vote (consistent with the cancelling neurons' references between the second-largest and the largest). Third-minus-second: ARC's two coefficients differ by a few percent by order (v19: equal).
- `oracle_halves.py`: ARC's per-order rules (36 numbers) are 99.96% right alone; with ARC's leftover from them borrowed, 88.6%: per-order typical behaviour is too coarse for the leftover to act as noise (per arrangement it was 94.1%). Mixing v19 with either half of ARC's per-order rules does not bring 'clean + noise' close to ARC (72-77%). Reading: an error model works only when the clean part matches ARC's typical behaviour at the level of individual arrangements; the fixes needed are fine-grained.

**Direction change (autonomous, 2026-10-18 night):** pause the error-model fitting and run the learned-dictionary comparison (standard tools): sparse autoencoders of several widths trained on ARC's hidden states, compared with the hand-built roles (v19, v21, v7z) and spliced back to measure faithfulness. ## Learned dictionaries: sparse autoencoders on ARC's states (`sae/`, 2026-10-18 night; autonomous)

- `train_sae.py`: TopK sparse autoencoders (ReLU encoder, top-k latents, unit-norm decoder; no dead-latent revival) on ARC's 16-neuron states, all steps, per-neuron scaling. Held-out unexplained variance: k = 2 5.9-9.1%, k = 4 1.9-3.0%, k = 8 0.4-0.5% across widths 16-128; dead latents up to 38 of 128 at k = 2.
- `eval_sae.py`, faithfulness: splicing the reconstruction into ARC breaks it. Last step only: 33-61% accurate (128 wide, k 8: 60.6%, 40% of ARC's mistakes); every step: 22-33%. Even 0.5% unexplained variance is fatal, as expected from the twentyfold cancellation.
- Roles: pooled correlations are inflated by components every unit shares (largest so far, current number, step). `eval_roles_fair.py` removes those first: ARC's own neurons match v19's roles at a mean best |correlation| of 0.86 (11 of 16 above 0.9), v21's 0.71, v7z's 0.73; SAE latents of every width and k match them equally or slightly worse (0.84-0.86, 0.70-0.73, 0.65-0.68). Learned dictionaries do not recover the hand-built roles better than the neurons do, and wider ones do not help.
- Reading (interpretation): for this network the standard sparse dictionary method neither finds the algorithm's roles nor yields a usable replacement; the bottleneck is precision, not sparsity. Caveats: one SAE recipe, no dead-latent revival, no transcoder (state-to-next-state) variant, correlation-based matching (no causal test yet).

## How many weight directions must be exact (`sensitive/restore.py`, 2026-10-19; Hilton's test at the weight level)

Sensitive directions: eigenvectors of the near-tie lead gradients (as `../hilton/hilton_lowrank.py`); the top 5 hold 80% of the sensitivity, the top 20 96%, the top 50 99%. Clean weights = ARC's rounded to a grid or with small weights zeroed; then ARC's values restored along the top k directions. Rounded to 0.01 (change RMS 0.003, about ARC's noise size): 90.2%; top 5 restored 94.1%, top 20 94.7% (82% of ARC's mistakes), top 50 95.2% (92%), top 100 95.2% (97%); 20 random directions 91.0% (57%). Rounded to 0.05: 61%; top 20 84%, top 50 93%, top 100 95% (85%). Rounded to 0.1: 26%; top 20 79%, top 50 88%, top 100 94% (72%). Weights under 0.1 zeroed: 46%; top 20 78%, top 100 94% (80%). Only 4-5% of each clean change lies in the top 20 directions, yet that part decides most of the accuracy. Reading: near ARC, about 5-20 directions carry its accuracy and 50-100 its specific mistakes; for genuinely clean weights the linear picture breaks and about 100 of 432 directions must keep ARC's values. Answer to Hilton's framing at the weight level: idealized weights plus gaps-as-noise work only if the idealized weights are within about ARC's own noise size, or if about a quarter of the weight directions are carried over exactly.
- `width/canc_vs_tolerance.py` (37 trained networks): the drop in accuracy from 0.5% noise on the final state is 23-48 points for 16-neuron networks, 31 for the 20-neuron one, 5-7 for the two 32-neuron ones; per-neuron cancellation predicts it only weakly (rank correlation 0.30 over all, 0.19 among 16-neuron networks; the least-cancelling 16-neuron network, ratio 16, drops the most, 48 points). ## Planted test: sparse autoencoders on hand-built networks with known roles (`planted/`, subagent, checked against its logs)

Same recipe as on ARC. v21 (28 neurons): unexplained 3.2-3.9% (k 2) to 0.2-0.6% (k 8); roles above 0.9 (shared parts removed): 8 of 26 at every width and k (the reveal group, acting as one signal, plus the pulse two steps after a record: in effect 2 roles); rotating groups and memory as subspaces: none of 5 above 0.9; spliced last step 16-52%, every step 10-18% (network 96%); random noise of the same size per neuron 32-48% and 17-21%. v19 (16): 0-1 of 14 roles (its clock phase is about 1e-8 of its neurons' variance, so a squared-error SAE ignores it), splicing at chance. v21 seen through a random 16-dimensional projection (more roles than dimensions): a linear map recovers 25 of 26 roles above 0.9, the SAE still 8. Controls: k up to 20 still 8-9 of 26; on a toy with 28 sparse features in 16 dimensions the recipe recovers 26 of 28. Reading: the roles in these networks are dense (a typical v21 state has 17 of 28 neurons on), which breaks the SAE's sparsity assumption; so ARC's weak role matches are not evidence that ARC lacks these roles, and splicing fails from the size of the error alone. One seed per setting; one judgement call (the largest so far before the current number was also removed as a shared quantity).

## Stiff and sloppy weight directions across networks (`stiff/stiff_across.py`, 2026-10-19)

Near-tie sensitivity matrix per network (leads in each network's votes). 16-neuron networks: 1-3 directions hold half the sensitivity, 7-19 hold 90%, 43-72 hold 99% of 432. Where a network has hiding neurons (switched off at a new largest on at least 85% of such steps; seven of eight 16-neuron networks), its top 5 directions put 43-72% of their weight on the recurrent weights into those neurons, against 11-22% of all weights (3-5 times over-represented); ARC 70% against 15%. 20 neurons: 5/27/106 directions, 41% against 13%. The 32-neuron networks differ: bo5 2/17/111 with only 1% on its hiding neurons, long 4/36/179 with 42% against 26%. Reading: in 16-neuron networks the stiff directions are few and sit on the hiding mechanism, supporting 'few stiff directions with mechanistic meaning, many sloppy ones'; the wide networks spread their sensitivity over more directions and, in one case, away from the hiding neurons.

### Level 2, noise response from explanation quantities (`level2/noise/`, subagent, checked against its logs)

Hilton's second question, scored on the shared truth (`level2/truth/noisy_truth.npz`). The account's structural constants computed from each copy's weights (hiding rotation and shrink, comparator gains, store ratio and leak, detector threshold, store's share of each comparator's reference, memory angles and decays, late gate weights; 35 numbers), propagated to first order in closed form without fitting, reach correlation 0.52 with the copies' accuracy (`span_greedy.log`; best single family 0.35); fitted by regression they reach 0.88 in-sample with 36-45 fitted numbers but transfer to other noise sizes at 0.70-0.81, worse than a constant guess in error (`screen.log`). Noise barely moves them (0.17 degrees of hiding rotation). The response sits in how much each neuron holds per unit of the largest two to six steps after it arrived (levels by age: 10 numbers 0.93, 16 numbers 0.96, 52 numbers 0.98), i.e. noise breaks the cancellation of the hidden largest's vote, not the switch points (steps near a comparator's switch carry 1-2% of the sensitivity). Three decision-level quantities read by running each copy on 720 designed lists (one per arrangement of the three largest): the runner-up's margin change per unit of the largest (runner-up before it, and after it) and of the third; a 3-number link fitted at s 0.0025 and applied unchanged: per-copy error 0.22 / 0.83 / 4.4 / 14.7 points, correlation 0.93 / 0.96 / 0.94 / 0.91 at s 0.001 / 0.0025 / 0.005 / 0.01 (level underpredicted at large noise); at equal cost better than sampling 720 lists up to s 0.0025. Two of them predict the share of answers naming the largest at about 0.8. Transfer to networks 106, 111, 115: refitted 0.85-0.93; ARC's coefficients unchanged keep the ranking (0.86-0.92) but are 2-8 points too low. Judgement call: the three quantities are named by the explanation but measured on designed lists, not computed from its formulas.

## Relation to the 'neural networks are not already clean' debate (L. Gao, LessWrong, 17 October 2026, replying to N. Cammarata; read 2026-10-19)

Gao argues that clean-looking activating examples do not mean understanding, that weak activations matter, and that the test is simulation scored by downstream KL. Our cases with known ground truth: ARC's neurons are clean in the sense of one-line switching rules and intervention-confirmed roles, yet every trained neuron fails simulation alone even at 99.8% of variance matched and all 16 leave 51% (`../gao/`); on a hand-built network that is clean by construction, a wrong reading scores 59-69% while the right idea with one gain 2% off scores 58% and the timer 0.5 degrees off 76% (robust version: 2% store error 71% against misreadings 69%), so a KL simulation score measures the precision of a description more than the cleanness of a neuron; and sparse dictionaries miss clean but always-active roles (planted test). Caveat: a 16-neuron recurrent toy, not language-model MLP neurons.
- Propagation line read (2026-10-19, `level2/switching/prop4.py`): a forward propagation of a probabilistic model of the hidden state with ARC's actual weights; exact running quantities (largest and second-largest so far on a grid of G equal-probability values, their positions, gate on/off), one Gaussian over the 16 neurons per combination, ReLU by exact Gaussian moments, inputs by a deterministic grid, final win probability by 4,096 fixed quasi-random points. Early numbers 95.84 / 95.46 / 95.28% at G 20 / 40 / 60 against 95.32%, with a tie-spacing setting (a third of a cell) that moves the result by about 0.3 points; frozen-settings held-out checks and an operation count requested. It is Hilton's mechanistic estimate with the explanation supplying the state's structure, not his faithfulness test with idealized weights (that version fails, gaps as large as the decisions). Cost by back-of-envelope: about 1e12 operations at G 60, roughly a thousand times sampling at equal error. **Decision (2026-10-19): a further subagent builds a much cheaper version that competes with sampling at equal compute (`level2/cheap/`).**

### Level 2, a cheap estimate against sampling (`level2/cheap/`, subagent, checked against `evaluate.log`)

Tie-line quadrature (`tlq.py`, `linelib.py`): for each arrangement of the three largest (all 720), move the second-largest between the third and the largest; the network is exactly linear between neuron switches, so the wrong stretches along the line are found exactly and integrated in closed form; only the two ends of each line are walked (97-99% of the wrong mass touches an end); the other values come from a fixed Sobol point set (deterministic). Settings frozen before evaluation. RMSE in accuracy points against plain sampling at the same operation count (one list = 5,920 operations): ARC 0.10 at 2,679 list-equivalents (seed luck; spread over point sets 0.24) vs sampling 0.41 and quasi-random whole lists 0.09; held-out seeds 100-115 0.40 vs 0.52 (quasi-random 0.45); at four times the cost 0.25 vs 0.26 (0.26); noisy copies s 0.0025 0.29 vs 0.50 (0.35), s 0.005 0.44 vs 0.58 (0.47). Earlier: `hilton/cheap` 0.82 at about 1,700 runs, `bias_formula_v4` 1.02 at about 53,000. Reading: a modest gain over plain sampling at low cost, which quasi-random sampling of whole lists mostly matches; at higher cost no gain; so this is good numerical integration, not mechanistic compression. What costs the error: the seven smaller numbers (they move the network's lean at a fixed arrangement by up to 13 times what the gradient predicts; simple features explain 31-85% of the lean's variance; one Gaussian state puts ARC's lean spread about 18 times too wide; coarsening the propagation to value grids of 6-24 points misses by up to 13 points). Side fact: with no biases, accuracy is identical for N(0, s²) inputs at any s.

## Why some neurons switch with no simple rule (2026-10-18, `norule/`, subagent)

Tested the idea that the rotating memory, read through ReLUs with no biases, leaves some neurons hovering near zero, and that this is why every network had 1-3 neurons whose on/off a depth-2 tree cannot predict. The census was the same as before, over 47 trained networks (39 of 16 neurons, one of 20, seven of 32). The answer is mixed. All 60 no-rule neurons are in the memory block. Those that clip often (on 65-90% of steps; 13 in 16-neuron networks) do hover: their pre-activation is a median 7% of its terms' summed size, against 25% for other memory neurons. They read the rotating subspace about twice as much (0.49 against 0.23) and the largest so far less (0.29 against 0.66). But 36 of the 49 no-rule neurons in 16-neuron networks are rarely clipping memory neurons (on at least 90%), tagged only because rare clips defeat a small tree. Most hovering neurons (34 of 58) are tagged "partial". 11 of 39 16-neuron networks have no no-rule neuron. Width lowers the share per neuron (8% to 5%) but not the count per network (1.3 at 16, 1.4 at 32). Making a no-rule neuron linear costs a median 0.45 points, less than other rarely clipping memory neurons (1.0). ARC's neuron 15 (7.3 points) and four others (5-10 points) are the exceptions. Whether missing biases force the hovering is untested; it would need networks trained with biases. Details: [norule/README.md](norule/README.md).

## Level 2 by forward propagation (`level2/switching/`, 2026-10-19, stopped)

Hilton's forward propagation, built as a mixture of Gaussians over the state. Each part is fixed by the positions and grid values of the largest and second-largest so far, plus the next-step on/off of three neurons switched by the previous number. The last number is kept exact at the readout. On ARC's actual weights the prediction is 95.67, 95.29, 95.10 and 94.98% at 20, 40, 60 and 80 grid points, against the true 95.32%. It is still drifting, with an extrapolated limit near 94.6%. With idealized weights and the gap treated as noise (Hilton's recipe proper) it gives 86.6% for weights rounded to 0.01, 46.4% at 0.05 and 50.9% for the block account. The propagation still tracks each idealized network's own accuracy within about 2 points. Unchanged, it is 2-15 points too low on four other trained networks. It costs roughly 1e12-1e13 operations, 1,000 to 100,000 times sampling at the same error. Details and what parts of the state matter: `level2/switching/README.md`.

## Level 2 by grid plus a model of the smaller numbers (`level2/smallnoise/`, 2026-10-18, done)

The grid over the three largest numbers, with the seven smaller numbers modelled instead of set to their mean. A Gaussian over all nine leads is about 2 points too pessimistic, because the leads over the smaller numbers are skewed. Keeping the fourth-largest exact and treating the other six as Gaussian is close to unbiased. Two frozen versions, accuracy RMSE in points: on 32 other trained networks the old grid 0.53, the first version 0.97 and a revised version 0.12 (0.12 on the 30 networks not used to choose it); on 200 noisy copies 0.54, 0.37 and 0.63; on the 100 shared copies 0.56, 0.32 and 0.54. Neither version wins everywhere. The remaining error is in lists where the fifth- to tenth-largest number wins, mostly when the third is negative, which ARC's family does often and other networks rarely. Mistake mix on ARC (largest / third / another): truth 20.4 / 63.4 / 16.2%, first version 20.7 / 63.9 / 15.3%. Cost: 171-445 million network runs per network; sampling reaches the same error with about 750-46,000 lists, so these are far more expensive than sampling. Reading: the seven smaller numbers can be modelled from the weights well enough to predict accuracy and its fall under noise to about half a point, but only by careful integration, not compression. Details: [level2/smallnoise/README.md](level2/smallnoise/README.md).

## Steering from the account (`steer/`, 2026-10-18, done)

Turning ARC's memory neurons up by 5% once at step 5 makes an earlier near-tie contender (stored in memory, largest arrived first) win less, 72% to 55%, the opposite of the account's prediction (87%). First-order bookkeeping explains it: at step 5 the memory carries the earlier contender's vote (+50) and a larger negative imprint of the largest (−83), while the store carries the largest (+87), which later discounts the later contender; the final lead is only about ±1.7. A balanced intervention from this corrected account (memory × (1+a), store × (1+0.96a)) moves decisions in the predicted direction in all 16 settings, by 60-100% of the predicted size upward and about half downward (step 5, second early, a +0.02: 85% against a predicted 84%). Side effect: turning down lets the largest win up to 7% (base 2%). Details: [steer/README.md](steer/README.md).
Three more steering tests (`steer/three.py`): hiding neurons turned down 10% at step 5 leak far less than the account predicts (largest wins 2.9% against 21%), because by then the largest's vote is cancelled jointly by hiding (+63), memory (−24) and store (−6); the detector scaled 5% at every step changes 11% of near-tie answers where the account predicts little change (untraced); the store turned up 1% at step 5 moves near-tie wins from 76% to 84%, and first-order bookkeeping predicts it within about a point.
Detector traced (`steer/detector_share.py`): a kept vote enters directly and through the detector one step later, about half each; a last-step number bypasses the detector, so nudging the detector moves last-step contenders' near-tie wins (58% to 67% or 48%) and little else. This explains the late-number bias. Turning down the last step's input by 1% removes the bias exactly (58% to 50.5%) but lowers random-list accuracy slightly (95.31% to 95.27%): the trained balance is near the best overall. Corrected account in five statements in `steer/README.md`.

## Interventions plan and results (`steer/PLAN.md`, `steer/RESULTS.md`, 2026-10-18)

Running table of 13 interventions with predictions written first: 7 wrong at first, all traced; the corrected account then predicted later tests. Highlights: a sensitivity map (every group, step, ±2%) is matched by first order everywhere; the largest's position is held only as a broad wave, so unhiding cannot make a largest-finder (10-16%); the late-number bias runs through the hiding neurons' last-step input, and turning that down 1% removes it (58% to 50%) at no net accuracy cost; edits keep their direction on all 20 noisy copies, at 50-75% of ARC's size.
Shift-register tests (`steer/phantom.py`): one extra step with input 0 makes ARC name the position one before the second on 94.5% of lists (its accuracy is 95.3%); appending an eleventh number twice the largest makes it name the old largest (shifted) on 95.9%, one between largest and second makes it name the last slot on 94.3%. The network behaves as if it had seen eleven numbers.
Votes can be slid continuously (`steer/fractional3.py`): the linear map of a 'forgotten number' step, raised to power 0.5 / 1 / 2, splits answers between the second and the position before it / names second−1 (79%) / names second−2 (77%). Backwards fails (17%): the step loses information. The rotating block alone does not shift (23%); the store's feed into it is needed every step. Rotation modes act like a Fourier code over positions: damping the 180° mode adds mistakes at distance 1 (54%), the 43° modes at distances 6-7 (`steer/modes.py`).

## What decides a near tie (`context/`, 2026-10-18, done)

Intervention attack on the lean that sets most mistakes. Each smaller number, when redrawn, flips 3-4% of near-tie decisions (all seven 17%); moving the largest flips about 27% of those moved. The smaller numbers' lead coefficients are heavy-tailed (median 0.31, 99th percentile 21, max 939) and positions explain under a fifth of their variation. No single neuron's switching makes the steep pieces (linearizing any one widens them). The comparators at the later contender's arrival predict the outcome barely above chance (AUC 0.52-0.57), and nudges there are no stronger than a step before or after. Per near tie the summed unit effects are about 760 times the lead, and a median of 11 (neuron, step) units could each flip it at a 5% nudge. Details: [context/README.md](context/README.md).
Near-tie rule from the account's variables (`context/learnable2.py`): positions plus the two top-three ratios predict the network's near-tie decision at 86% (base 79%, ceiling about 90% from the top three), but only with about 25,000 tree numbers; up to 400 numbers stay at the base rate. The network (432 weights) is the shortest description found. Permanent steering (`steer/permanent.py`): every-step neuron scaling equals an outgoing-weight edit, and readout edits are exact; the detector's outgoing weights x1.04 remove the late-number bias at 0.44 points of accuracy, and readout x the step map makes a network that names the position before the second (79%).
Special inputs, property style (`special/README.md`): scale changes never change the answer; increasing lists name 8, decreasing name 1, two spikes are right at all 90 placements on zero, positive or negative backgrounds (the account wrongly expected negative backgrounds to hurt); all-negative lists fall back to positions 0-1; a repeated list makes it name the largest (90-94%), because an exact copy of the largest splits the answer between the two copies and the older copy is outside the window.
Near-tie lean has no simple components (`context/components.py`): 12 named terms in a logistic model reach the base rate (79.7% against 79.3%); neuron 7's final push alone predicts nothing. Hypothesis-shrunk counterexamples (`special/README.md`): a resolution limit of about 3% between the largest and second (two spikes right at every placement down to a ratio of 1.05), records barely above each other, and accuracy falling as largest/second grows (97.8% at 1.1-1.5 to 81.8% above 10).
Most confident mistakes (`extreme/README.md`): with 20% gaps the network errs on 0.14% of lists. Negative numbers act as noise proportional to their size (scaling only the negatives: 96.3% at x0, 96.0% at x1, 93.0% at x4). Weak shapes: the second at position 0 overtaken at step 9 (4.1% wrong) or 7 (1.2%), not explained by fresh records or the cold start; an adjacent earlier third beats the second ten times more often than an adjacent later one.
Slot 0's weakness is carried by the 126° rotation mode: removing 20% of it fixes those lists (4.1% -> 0.0%) but costs 2.3 points elsewhere (`extreme/slot0_mode.py`). Monotonicity (`special/monotone.py`): raising the second, or lowering the third or a smaller number, rarely breaks a right answer (0.02-0.13%) except inside the comparators' resolution zone (second at 0.9-0.95 of the largest: 6%) and at near ties (0.64%), where a small change in any number can flip the answer either way; hypothesis found such paradoxes (raising the second makes the third win).
Robustness and composition (`extreme/pgd.py`, `extreme/compose.py`): a gradient search moving the smaller numbers by up to 3% of the second flips 0.6% of right answers (near ties 3.9%), by 30% flips 4% (near ties 25%); first-order worst cases overstate this, switching protects. With the top two in one half and the other half quiet the network is right 98.5-99.8%; lists whose top two come late are wrong about three times as often as lists whose top two come early (cause untraced; one guess, the revealed early record, rejected).
Early-versus-late asymmetry (`asym/README.md`): not one mechanism. Accuracy by (second, largest) position is 96-99.7% except a few weak cells (largest at the last step: 92% on average; largest first with a late second: 87-91%), which reproduce the gap (late top two 96.0%, early 98.9%; half from the last-step largest). Each weak cell is fixed by removing a different rotation mode (0°/126°, 180°/83°, 43°, or none), and the error lands at a specific nearby position. The trained mode balance suits the average placement; no setting suits every one.
Morph and average (`morph/README.md`, after Obermeyer et al., Nature 2026, run on inputs since every list is valid): gradient morphs of the smaller numbers flip only 37.5% of right near ties; per arrangement the mean morph is stable (split-half cosine 0.97) but specific: shared position profiles explain 25% of it, single morphs share only 39% with their arrangement's mean. Reliable, not compressible.
Width of the uncertain zone (`width_band/README.md`): the third wins 44% of lists at a tie, 25% at a 1-2% gap, 11% at 3-5%, 1.3% at 12-20%; largest against second is narrower (about 1-2%). From the weights, with the smaller numbers as Gaussian noise through one linear map per arrangement, the curve is within 2-3 points inside the zone but too wide beyond it (lost close calls 4.6% predicted, 2.9% true; 4.0% with maps at six gaps): switching damps the context's effect, which a linear map misses.
Continued training (`finetune/README.md`): a copy of ARC trained further gains a point quickly (95.3% to 96.0-96.4% in 10,000 steps), narrowing the uncertain zone's sides (third wins at 3-8% gaps 7.5% to 4.0%), almost entirely outside the 20 sensitive directions. The close calls get rarer but stay dense: lead maps covering half the lists 9,040 to 5,532 (hand-built 46-48); a tree on the top three still predicts near ties far worse than for the hand-built networks.
Siblings (`siblings/README.md`): all 39 trained 16-neuron networks, the 20-neuron one and seven 32-neuron ones have dense close calls (1,400-36,000 lead maps covering half, a tree on the top three reaching 3-39% of the possible gain), against 46-48 maps and 84-91% for the hand-built networks. ARC is typical; wider networks make fewer close-call mistakes, not simpler ones.
Training from a hand-built network (`train_from_handbuilt/README.md`, approved exception): the fragile 16-neuron one breaks at once and recovers dense; the robust 28-neuron one rises from 95.9% to 98.3% in 3,000 steps while its close calls stop being predictable from the top three (tree's share of the gain 84% to 20%, the trained range), though its lead maps grow only to about 200-300. Clean close calls are not a resting point for training: context-dependent corrections raise accuracy, and nothing rewards simplicity.
Noise control (`noise_control/README.md`): the robust hand-built network with weight noise that brings it to ARC's accuracy keeps about 55 lead maps (ARC about 8,000) and near-tie decisions a top-three tree explains to within 2% (ARC 14%); even at 79-86% accuracy it stays far simpler. Dense is not just imprecise.
v23, ARC's corrected mechanism built from scratch (`v23/README.md`): 16 neurons, every weight from a rule, 95.8% accurate, 47 lead maps (ARC about 8,000). Switching like ARC's adds few maps; a vote leak spread over all positions (what nine positions in eight memory dimensions leave with ARC's angles) gives about 2,000 maps but costs accuracy uncompensated. Density is not forced by the mechanism; looping the memory back is still unsolved (costs 11-25 points).
