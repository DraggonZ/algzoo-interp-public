# Which trained networks are claimed to be fully understood?

Checked 2026-10-17. Read-only: nothing was posted or installed. Each quote below was copied from the primary source today, unless it says it was verified in an earlier note in this folder. "Our network" means ARC's 16-neuron second-largest network (M16,10), 432 parameters, 95.3% accurate.

## Summary

The proposed claim does not hold as worded. ARC's own post has two smaller trained networks from the same family, with 2 and 4 neurons. ARC says it fully understands both, and for each it built a network by hand with the same number of neurons. Their evidence is stronger than interventions: an accounting of every linear region, and accuracy estimates that roughly match random sampling. So "tested by a hand-built rebuild of the same size" is not rare in this exact family.

What is rare is narrower. Our network is the largest second-largest network with a same-size hand-built network. ARC's own build for it uses 22 neurons, and Chen's uses 21. But our 98.9% build uses its own algorithm, and the copy of the trained network's neuron roles is still incomplete. A same-size build with a different algorithm shows the task fits in 16 neurons. It does not test our account of the trained network.

Across the literature, "fully understood" means one of four things, from weakest to strongest:

1. **The algorithm is named and confirmed by interventions.** Examples are modular addition (Nanda et al. 2023) and group composition (Chughtai et al. 2023). Ablations confirm it, but no accuracy is derived from the account.
2. **Replacing parts by the hypothesis keeps most of the loss.** Causal scrubbing on a parenthesis checker recovered 88–93% of the loss. IOI uses similar faithfulness tests.
3. **A proof or bound of accuracy follows from the account.** Examples are Max-of-K (Gross et al. 2024) and group operations (Wu et al. 2024). Even here, bounds get looser as the proof uses more of the explanation.
4. **ARC's standard.** Surprise accounting gives full understanding, and an estimate that matches sampling's error at equal compute is the practical test. Only the 10-parameter and 32-parameter AlgZoo networks meet it, and ARC is less sure about the 32-parameter one.

Networks explained "end to end" are all small: one-layer transformers or MLPs with a few hundred neurons, or recurrent networks with 1 to about 20 units. We found no source that claims full understanding of a recurrent network with as many as 16 densely connected ReLU neurons. The hidden sizes of the recurrent networks in the program-synthesis paper were not checked. Our network meets none of the four standards fully. It meets the first for its main mechanism, and the third and fourth not at all.

## What this means for our claim

**Same-size rebuilds already exist in this family.** ARC's code builds the 2-neuron and 4-neuron networks by hand at the trained size. ARC's analysis of those two networks is also more complete than ours.

**Interventions plus a rebuild is a fair description of our evidence, but common in parts.** Interventions are standard (Nanda, Chughtai, IOI, causal scrubbing). Rebuilds of trained circuits are rarer: Curve Circuits rebuilt one circuit, and Gromov wrote weights for a whole small MLP. The combination of interventions and a same-size rebuild of a whole recurrent network looks uncommon. We have not proven it is rare, and our search could miss neuroscience work.

**The rebuild tests our account only if it copies the trained mechanism.** The 98.9% build uses its own algorithm. It shows that 16 neurons are enough. It does not show that the trained network works as we say. The role-by-role copy would test that, but it is incomplete. A reviewer from ARC would point to their own same-size rebuilds and to their standard, an estimate that matches sampling, which ours misses.

## Verdict

The proposed sentence, "one of the few trained networks whose account has been tested both by interventions and by a hand-built rebuild of the same size", should not be used. It is contradicted in part by ARC's 2- and 4-neuron networks. It also implies the rebuild tests our account, which holds only for the incomplete role-by-role copy.

Safe wording:

> "Our account of M16,10 rests on interventions with random-neuron controls. We also give a hand-built network of the same size, 16 neurons, that does the task at 98.9% with its own algorithm. ARC built same-size networks for its 2- and 4-neuron models, but its build for M16,10 needs 22 neurons. A hand-built copy of the trained network's own neuron roles is still incomplete. Our accuracy estimate does not match sampling, so M16,10 is not fully understood in ARC's sense."

If the role-by-role copy is finished and works at 16 neurons, a stronger sentence becomes defensible: "To our knowledge, this is the first hand-built network that copies the trained M16,10 neuron by neuron at its own size." That still needs the caveat about ARC's standard.

## Limits of this search

- Sources were found by memory and targeted search, not by citation tracking or Google Scholar. A closer case could exist, especially in neuroscience.
- Gromov (2023) was read only in its abstract. Whether it uses interventions was not checked.
- Model sizes for the Nanda, Gross, Wu, Stander and Othello papers come from their method sections. The size for Quirke and Barez comes from a figure caption (3 heads); its width was not checked.
- MIPS simplifies each network before extracting code, so the code describes the simplified network.
