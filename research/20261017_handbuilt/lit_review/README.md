# Literature review: ARC's 16-neuron second-argmax network (M16,10) and related work

Checked 2026-10-17. Read-only search: nothing was posted or installed. Every source below was opened at the primary source unless it is listed under "Could not verify". Quotes are copied from the source. "We" and "our" mean the paper draft `paper/draft_v2.md`.

## Summary

Nobody has published a full explanation of M16,10, and nobody has met ARC's challenge. On 17 August 2026 Jacob Hilton saw no specific effort outside the post's comments, and "not much closer to succeeding at the challenge".

Partial accounts of the same network exist, and some are close to ours:

- **The cancelling neurons.** Zihao Chen's 2024 ARC notes say that removing neurons 1, 6, 7 and 8 from the readout leaves a circuit that finds the largest's position. They also say a new largest gives the old largest's score "a boost". That is the core of our hidden timer, seen from the readout side but without interventions. Goldowsky-Dill (2026) states that a new largest resets those four neurons to zero.
- **Martin Keane** published about 50 notes in a public fork (January–March 2026). They cover a "phase wheel", the four neurons' reset and rebuild, neuron 5's role after the largest, and near-tie errors. Nearly all use two-number lists or uniform inputs. His "accuracy estimate" covers only 90 two-number lists.
- **Hand-built networks.** ARC built a 22-neuron network and Chen a 21-neuron one, both 99%. Neither matches the trained network's size or mechanism.
- **Accuracy estimate.** ARC's analysis of the 10-parameter network is the template: error is the share of inputs between the decision boundary and the tie line. A 2026 preprint (Mariappan) applied a crude version to M16,10, without comparing to sampling.

What looks new: interventions with random-neuron controls on random lists, the gate's threshold, a 16-neuron rebuild mirroring the trained mechanism, close-call biases measured across 22 networks, and an estimate scored against sampling on many networks.

One caution: our estimate runs the network along fixed lines. Whether ARC would call that "mechanistic" is a judgement call.

## Annotated bibliography

### 1. AlgZoo, the post, its comments and follow-up work

- **Hilton, J. (2026). AlgZoo: uninterpreted models with fewer than 1,500 parameters.** ARC blog, 26 January 2026; cross-posted to [LessWrong and the Alignment Forum](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/x8BbjZqooS4LFXS8Z/algzoo-uninterpreted-models-with-fewer-than-1-500-parameters) (post `x8BbjZqooS4LFXS8Z`, 10 comments). [Blog](https://www.alignment.org/blog/algzoo-uninterpreted-models-with-fewer-than-1-500-parameters/). It defines the challenge and the standard. It describes neurons 2, 4, 6, 7 and 1 as leave-one-out maximum features. It also builds a 22-neuron network by hand. It names Zihao Chen, George Robinson, David Matolcsi, Jacob Stavrianos, Jiawei Li and Michael Sklar as contributors. Quotes: "we would consider it a major research breakthrough if we were able to produce a mechanistic estimate that matched the performance of random sampling under the mean squared error versus compute metric." Also: "we can construct a model by hand that uses 22 hidden neurons to form all 10 leave-one-out-maximum features, and leverages these to achieve an accuracy of 99%." Also: "we have not produced a correspondence between most of hidden neurons of the trained model and the hidden neurons of the handcrafted model." For the 10-parameter network: "the error rate is the fraction of the unit disk lying between the model's decision boundary and the line x0=x1". Footnotes: "An estimate for a single model could be close by chance, so the method should match sampling on average over random seeds." And: "A better but more expensive way to measure mean squared error is to instead average over random seeds used to train the model." The second footnote supports our use of many trained networks.
- **Hilton, J. Comments on the AlgZoo post** (4 February and 17 August 2026). They show that ARC's standard asks for estimates derived from the weights. They also show that no one has met the challenge. Quotes: "You'd need to do things like deduce correlations from the weights, rather than just observe them empirically". And: "I don't recall seeing any specific effort on these models in the last six months outside of the comments on this post. To me, that progress is fairly marginal and we are not much closer to succeeding at the challenge given at the end of the post."
- **Goldowsky-Dill, N. (2026). Comment, 28 January 2026, with [gist](https://gist.github.com/NixGD/2651be79bfd22bcb13d1f609f23439a4) and repository [NixGD/rnn-explaining](https://github.com/NixGD/rnn-explaining)** (commits 28 January to 10 February 2026). He fits neurons 1, 6, 7 and 8 as linear in the running max and second max, per cell of "steps since max, steps since second". He uses Uniform(−1, 1) inputs. Quote from the comment: "There are also parts of the task that I don't know how the model does, e.g. tracking the current position of the 2nd-maximum value". From the repository README: "Case 2 | `x_t > M` | New max: `dm → 1`, neurons N1,6,7,8 reset to 0". From the notebook: "the large-negative input weights w_ih[n] ≈ −10 to −13 cause ReLU to kill neurons".
- **Keane, M. (2026). Comments, 31 January to 5 February 2026; [gist](https://gist.github.com/mrsirrisrm/d6850ff8647d1ed2f67cc92d5bce3ed0); fork [mrsirrisrm/alg-zoo](https://github.com/mrsirrisrm/alg-zoo)** (49 commits to 23 March 2026, docs 01–49). This is the most extensive prior analysis of M16,10. Most experiments use two inputs of 1.0 and 0.8 with zeros elsewhere. Random-list experiments use U[0,1] inputs, where he measures 89% accuracy. Quotes from the comments: "Zeroing out all the inputs except the largest two gets the network to 100%". Also: "There appear to be two different ways in which the output is encoded, depending on whether the 2nd largest input comes before or after the largest." Also: "The hidden state follows a spiral trajectory through time, implemented by a rotating phase in the hidden state. The W_out projection converts phase angle to position logits." Fork doc 25: "Both impulses clip comps to zero. The rebuild trajectory after the last clip encodes position." Doc 11: "n5 is essential for the single-impulse regime where 2nd_argmax comes after argmax and doesn't clip." Doc 12: "When 2nd and 3rd values are very close (gap < 0.03), the error rate is **26%**". Doc 42 ("Mechanistic Accuracy Estimate from Weights") works on 90 two-number lists only: "84% (76/90 pairs) via linear phase wheel mechanism". Doc 48: "To predict even the **sign** of the margin (correct vs wrong), you need accuracy better than 0.1% in the S-A residual." Doc 30, across five zoo seeds: "The specific tropical cell structure and neuron role assignments are ONE valid solution, not THE solution." The gist: "The ReLU activation pattern D[t] is entirely determined by canonical sequences precomputed from weights".
- **Chen, Z. (2024). RNNs for the 2nd argmax.** ARC note, April 2024, linked from the post. [PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1USCD1LPa6hXMTj7PAO6Jmc4dVdxemZY3/view). Chen originally trained M16,10, which he reports at 94% on uniform inputs. This is the closest prior work to our timer claim. Quotes: "projecting out the negative input neurons (N1, N6, N7, N8) gives an argmax/max sub-circuit that accurately finds the max index." Also: "For each iteration t, if x_t > max_t (running max value), the old max logit gets a boost." Also: "The constructed 21-neuron net has an accuracy 99%." And: "the trained net has 4 negative input neurons which seem very entangled and this has been a major obstacle to understanding how (s_k − m_k) is represented."
- **Hilton, J. Heuristic explanations for 2nd argmax models; Robinson, G. (2024). Symmetric RNNs.** ARC notes linked from the post ([Hilton](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1t2rXhDQqWrOXRwjqstbrBPWiCmYlMts_/view), [Robinson](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qH61_WshTrgYh0YLj5vkONzk8x00zImN/view)). Both cover the 32-parameter network: symmetries, then a region-by-region check. They are background for "brute force after exploiting symmetries". Robinson quote: "RNNs of this size with the symmetries above are generically equivalent to a (3,2,3) neural network with activation function given by absolute value." Hilton's note was only partly readable as text (formulas).
- **Mariappan, K. K. (2026). Causal Geometry of an Alg-Zoo RNN: A Mechanistic Case Study.** Preprint PDF dated 8 March 2026 in [datasci3nce/causal-geometry-algzoo](https://github.com/datasci3nce/causal-geometry-algzoo). Not peer-reviewed. Its "mechanistic error bound" applies the near-tie idea to the largest–second gap only. The width is found by a 12-step search on sampled lists, and it is not compared to sampling. Quotes: "We derive a mechanistic error bound of 3.44% from the manifold's geometric resolution limit". And: "We empirically measured this limit via a binary search along the decision boundary, finding a worst-case threshold of δ ≈ 0.0227." It compares against "the model's observed empirical error of 3.2% – 4.7%".
- **Brito, M. (2026). [dinamicoplus/algzoo-legibility](https://github.com/dinamicoplus/algzoo-legibility).** One commit, 16 October 2026: scripts that ablate weak activations and swap neurons for fitted labels. It appears to respond to the clean-neurons debate. It has no write-up. README: "A quick, informal experiment on ARC's 432-parameter AlgZoo model".
- **Christiano, P. Comment on "Returning to ARC"**, 5 August 2026 ([post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vLFh8HP3hyNy9MCwe/returning-to-arc)). Quote: "explaining the AlgZoo models would be a huge milestone for us. (I don't think any of the mechanistic interpretability approaches can handle any of these models either".
- **Other mentions, background only.** Leo Gao's shortform suggests "fully interpreting the algzoo models, possibly using circuit sparsity techniques" (22 July 2026). Mike Winer of ARC: "I would guess that for a lot of simple networks (eg the ones in AlgZoo), estimating wouldn't be that much harder than for random MLPs" (17 August 2026). Logan Riggs, ["Mech Interp is a Verifiable Task If..."](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QxHuKtfGfzn8uokoR/mech-interp-is-a-verifiable-task-if) (21 September 2026): "small models trained on algorithmic tasks (algzoo) which already exhibit many behaviors we don't know how to approach."
- **AlgZoo repository** ([alignment-research-center/alg-zoo](https://github.com/alignment-research-center/alg-zoo)). It has no issues, pull requests or discussions, and five forks. An `mlp` branch (19 August 2026) adds "handcrafted kth argmax MLP". The README says zoo models are "the best of 5 random seeds".

### 2. ARC's theory of mechanistic estimates

- **Neyman, E., Lecomte, V., Wu, W., Winer, M., Hilton, J., Robinson, G. (2025). [Competing with sampling](https://www.alignment.org/blog/competing-with-sampling/).** ARC blog, 18 November 2025. It defines the matching sampling principle and what "mechanistic" means. It matters for judging whether our estimate qualifies. Quote: "loosely speaking, we mean that G_M estimates the expected output of M_θ deductively, based on the structure of M_θ. This contrasts with sampling-based algorithms for estimating the expected output of M_θ, which operate based on inductive reasoning."
- **Hilton, J. (2024). [Formal verification, heuristic explanations and surprise accounting](https://www.alignment.org/blog/formal-verification-heuristic-explanations-and-surprise-accounting/).** ARC blog, 25 June 2024. It is the second yardstick in the AlgZoo post. Quote: "The surprise of the explanation itself: how many times did the explanation just "work out" for no apparent reason?"
- **Christiano, P., Neyman, E., Xu, M. (2022). [Formalizing the presumption of independence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06738).** Our estimate treats tie biases as effectively random, which is a presumption-of-independence step. Quote: "the use of a "default" estimate of E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] in the absence of any specific information about the correlation between X and Y, which we call *the presumption of independence*."
- **Christiano, P., Hilton, J., Lincoln, A., Neyman, E., Xu, M. (2024). [Towards a Law of Iterated Expectations for Heuristic Estimators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01290).** Our estimate is too low for 21 of 22 networks, which this principle counts as a defect. Quote: "a heuristic estimator ought not to be able to predict its own errors".
- **Wu, G., Hilton, J. (2024). [Estimating the Probabilities of Rare Outputs in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13211)**; blog post by Wu, 18 October 2024. It is the empirical precedent for comparing estimators with naive sampling. Quote: "We find that importance sampling outperforms activation extrapolation, but both outperform naive sampling."
- **Xu, M. (2024). [Estimating Tail Risk in Neural Networks](https://www.alignment.org/blog/estimating-tail-risk-in-neural-networks/).** ARC blog. Background motivation. Quote: "We are excited about techniques to estimate the probability of tail events that do not rely on finding inputs on which an AI behaves badly".
- **Wu, W., Lecomte, V., Winer, M., Robinson, G., Hilton, J., Christiano, P. (2026). [Estimating the expected output of wide random MLPs more efficiently than sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05179).** ARC's first estimator that beats sampling, on random networks only. Quote: "for sufficiently wide networks, our estimator achieves a target mean squared error using substantially fewer FLOPs than Monte Carlo sampling."
- **Hilton, J., Christiano, P., Wu, W. (2026). [Announcing the ARC White-Box Estimation Challenge](https://www.alignment.org/blog/announcing-the-arc-white-box-estimation-challenge/).** 2 June 2026. ARC's current bet is to start from random networks, because trained ones are hard. Quote: "designing highly performant white-box estimation methods for trained networks is challenging, even for tiny models."
- **Neyman, E. (2025). [A computational no-coincidence principle](https://www.alignment.org/blog/a-computational-no-coincidence-principle/).** Background only. Quote: "If an apparently outrageous coincidence happens in mathematics, then there is a reason for it."

### 3. Proofs and verified explanations of small models

- **Gross, J., Agrawal, R., Kwa, T., Ong, E., Yip, C. H., Gibson, A., Noubir, S., Chan, L. (2024). [Compact Proofs of Model Performance via Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11779).** Accuracy bounds for a max-of-K transformer across 151 seeds. It is the closest published analogue to "estimate accuracy from mechanism". Its main obstacle matches our mistakes finding. Quotes: "more faithful mechanistic understanding leads to tighter performance bounds" and "we identify compounding structureless errors as a key challenge".
- **Wu, W., Jaburi, L., Drori, J., Gross, J. (2024). [Towards a unified and verified understanding of group-operation networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07476).** ARC's post cites it as prior full understanding. Quote: "We verify that our explanation applies to a large fraction of networks trained on this task by translating it into a compact proof of model performance".
- **Yip, C. H., Agrawal, R., Chan, L., Gross, J. (2024). [Modular addition without black-boxes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03773).** It compresses a ReLU layer instead of brute-forcing it. Quote: "target a non-vacuous bound on the behaviour of the ReLU MLP in time linear in the parameter-count of the circuit."
- **Sotoudeh, M., Thakur, A. V. (2019). [Computing Linear Restrictions of Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06214).** This is prior art for our exact walk along a line. Quote: "ExactLine computes a partitioning of the given input line segment such that the network is affine on each partition." They also note that sampling misses decision boundaries, as our search by halving did.
- **Palumbo, N., et al. (2024). [Validating Mechanistic Interpretations: An Axiomatic Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13594).** It frames an explanation as something that must reproduce the network. Quote: "a description that approximately captures the semantics of the neural network under analysis in a compositional manner."

### 4. Small recurrent networks and algorithmic tasks

- **Sussillo, D., Barak, O. (2013). Opening the black box: low-dimensional dynamics in high-dimensional recurrent neural networks.** Neural Computation 25(3):626–649, [PubMed 23272922](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23272922/). Fixed-point analysis. Our network is driven by inputs and is far from a fixed point, so we do not use it. Quote: "the mechanisms of trained networks could be inferred from the sets of fixed and slow points and the linearized dynamics around them."
- **Maheswaranathan, N., Williams, A., Golub, M., Ganguli, S., Sussillo, D. (2019). [Reverse engineering recurrent networks for sentiment classification reveals line attractor dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10720).** Quote: "trained networks converge to highly interpretable, low-dimensional representations."
- **Maheswaranathan, N., et al. (2019). [Universality and individuality in neural dynamics across large populations of recurrent networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08549).** Related to our variation across networks. Quote: "while the geometry of neural dynamics can vary greatly across architectures, the underlying computational scaffold ... often appears universal".
- **Turner, E., Dabholkar, K., Barak, O. (2021). [Charting and navigating the space of solutions for recurrent neural networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09356).** It is the closest RNN precedent for two of our points: different trained networks find different solutions, and perturbation reveals hidden structure. Quote: "a perturbation to a system often reveals hidden structure".
- **Michaud, E. J., et al. (2024). [Opening the AI black box: program synthesis via mechanistic interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05110).** Automated extraction of algorithms from small RNNs. Quote: "MIPS uses an integer autoencoder to convert the RNN into a finite state machine".
- **Nanda, N., Chan, L., Lieberum, T., Smith, J., Steinhardt, J. (2023). [Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217).** The best-known rotation ("clock") mechanism. Our dial is a related rotation over time. - **Zhong, Z., Liu, Z., Tegmark, M., Andreas, J. (2023). [The Clock and the Pizza](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844).** Supports "only some networks share the trick". Quote: "Small changes to model hyperparameters and initializations can induce the discovery of qualitatively different algorithms".
- **Chughtai, B., Chan, L., Nanda, N. (2023). [A Toy Model of Universality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03025).** Quote: "for a given network the precise circuits learned -- as well as the order they develop -- are arbitrary."
- **Li, Y., Yosinski, J., Clune, J., Lipson, H., Hopcroft, J. (2015). [Convergent Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07543).** Quote: "some features are learned reliably in multiple networks, yet other features are not consistently learned".

### 5. Hidden, cancelled or dormant signals

- **Kaufman, M. T., Churchland, M. M., Ryu, S. I., Shenoy, K. V. (2014). Cortical activity in the null space: permitting preparation without movement.** Nature Neuroscience 17(3):440–448, [PubMed 24487233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24487233/). This is the closest conceptual match to a timer that is cancelled at the readout until released. Quote: "changes in motor cortical activity cancel out at the level of these population readouts."
- **Makelov, A., Lange, G., Nanda, N. (2023). [Is This the Subspace You Are Looking for?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030).** It is a known way for patching to mislead, close to our interchange caution. Quote: "this effect may be achieved by activating a dormant parallel pathway leveraging another subspace that is causally disconnected from model outputs."
- **McGrath, T., Rahtz, M., Kramár, J., Mikulik, V., Legg, S. (2023). [The Hydra Effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771).** It shows that compensating parts confuse ablation. Quote: "ablations of one attention layer of a language model cause another layer to compensate".
- **Keane (2026), fork doc 47.** In the same network he reports two large signals that nearly cancel at the readout. This differs from ours: it is a split of the matrix into symmetric and antisymmetric parts, not a group of neurons. Quote: "The network's actual discrimination margin (~10) emerges from **massive cancellation** between these two signals (~3000-4000 each)".

### 6. Hand-built networks as ground truth or as tests

### 7. Testing explanations: completeness, faithfulness, ablation choices

## What contradicts or weakens our claims

- **The timer claim is less new than the draft implies.** Chen's note already saw it from the readout side, and Goldowsky-Dill saw the reset. The draft's "What was known" omits Chen. Our addition is the causal evidence and the cancellation picture.
- **Is our estimate "mechanistic"?** ARC defines mechanistic as deductive from structure, and Hilton asks for correlations derived "from the weights, rather than just observe[d] ... empirically". Our walk is exact along chosen lines. But it fixes the smaller numbers and assumes those lines are representative, and that step is empirical. ARC's own 10-parameter analysis also checks linear pieces one by one, so the walk is in spirit. The fixed-filler step is a judgement call that a reviewer from ARC might reject.
- **Systematic bias.** Our estimate is low for 21 of 22 networks. ARC's theory says a good estimator should not be able to predict its own errors (Christiano et al. 2024). The draft states the bias, which is right. It should not present the estimate as unbiased.
- **The noisy-copy test.** ARC suggests testing on noisy copies of M16,10. Our estimate misses by 1–3 points there, much worse than on trained networks. ARC's footnote calls averaging over training seeds "better but more expensive", which supports our main test. The noisy-copy result is still the one ARC named.
- **Keane's differential-amplifier result** says margins are tiny leftovers of large cancelling signals, so any approximation breaks them. This supports "mistakes resist explanation" but predates it. Cite it as agreement, not as our discovery.
- **Generic effects.** Sutter et al. show that unrestricted nonlinear maps fit anything. Our nonlinear-reader result is meaningful only beside the hand-built baseline, which the draft already has.
- **Unverified rival claim.** A LessWrong user ("phoenix") claims a private tool that reads M16,10's accuracy exactly from the weights: "The certificate closes at 0.953019 == MC 0.953019". The wording suggests it checks against Monte Carlo on the same sample, which would not be an independent estimate. The repository is private. If the claim were real and mechanistic, it would beat our estimate. It cannot be checked, so do not cite it as evidence. Possibly mention it as an unverified claim.
- **Input distributions differ between studies.** Goldowsky-Dill and Chen used Uniform(−1, 1), and Keane used U[0,1] (89% accuracy). Their numbers are not directly comparable with our Gaussian 95.3%.
