## Log

- Part 1: store and detector checked exact against the true running maximum on 100,000 random sequences and on hand-picked ones.
- Before part 2, an idealized vote model (`scripts/ideal_votes.py`) showed that if every smaller number kept a vote and votes added up, a one-frequency clock gives 24–43% and even a kernel as sharp as ARC's about 88%. Measuring ARC's model (`trained_vote_kernel.py`, `trained_vote_erase.py`, `trained_vote_jacobian.py`) showed it keeps lasting votes only for numbers that arrived as a new largest or new second. That set design v1.
- Part 2: second-largest store exact on 200,000 sequences; gates exact outside the designed 1% band.
- Part 3: a lone input leaves all scores equal at every position and age. v1 random accuracy 86.6%. Failures inspected one by one (session log in chat): the last input's cosine-shaped vote and the stored vote added up on a slot between them. v2 fixed the readout.
- v2: mistake table against ARC's model (`check_v2.py`): every v2 mistake other than "only one positive input" is a near-tie within 1%.
- ARC's model, follow-up (`trained_erase_mechanism.py`, `trained_wipe_threshold.py`, `arc_23_order.py`): a number below the second leaves almost no trace one step later; above about 0.8 of the second it switches some of the lowering neurons off and its trace lasts. ARC's second–third mix-ups happen in all arrival orders at similar rates. (The 'soft switch' reading drawn from this was later corrected; see next entries.)
- ARC's model, correction (`arc_third_vote.py`, `arc_neuron5.py`): the third-largest's lasting vote is all or nothing, switching at about 0.6–0.85 of the second (varies by input); neuron 5, one step after arrival, fits 14.6 x − 11.6 × second (R² 0.91). - Exact flip points (`exact_flip.py`, `exact_flip.log`; can the bisection brute force be avoided?). The network is piecewise linear along the line that varies the third number, so one forward pass with a tangent gives the margin and its slope; solve, and step past any neuron that switches on the way. On ARC's 5,040 second–third flips: 2.4 passes on average (65% need one), against 24 bisection steps; agreement to 1e-8 on 98%; 5% unresolved within 20 passes and 1.6% land on a different crossing (margin crosses zero more than once), which would need a fallback. A pass with tangent costs about two runs, so about 5 times cheaper: roughly 27,000 run-equivalents instead of 138,240. Not enough to beat sampling: the estimate's 0.73-point floor beats sampling only below about 1,200 runs, and the 720 placements × 8 settings, not the search, are now the cost.
