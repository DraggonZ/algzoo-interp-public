# Prior art for the run-3 techniques (checked 2026-10-17)

Each entry was opened at its arXiv abstract page; quotes are verbatim from the abstract.

- **Unembedding null space (technique 1, hidden-signal test).** Stolfo, Wu, Gurnee, Belinkov, Song, Sachan, Nanda, "Confidence Regulation Neurons in Language Models" ([arXiv 2406.16254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16254)): "Our work shows that entropy neurons operate by writing onto an unembedding null space". Cancedda, "Spectral Filters, Dark Signals, and Attention Sinks" ([arXiv 2402.09221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09221)): "the signals exchanged in the tail end of the spectrum are responsible for attention sinking." So functional signals in directions the output cannot read are known in language models. What run 3 adds is the erase test: which events turn a hidden part into a visible one.
- **Successor heads (technique 2, shift test).** Gould, Ong, Ogden, Conmy, "Successor Heads: Recurring, Interpretable Attention Heads In The Wild" ([arXiv 2312.09230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09230)): "attention heads that increment tokens with a natural ordering, such as numbers, months, and days", underlain by "a set of 'mod-10 features'". Shift-like structure is known in language models; the weight identity for a recurrent step is specific to RNNs and state-space models.
- **Matching units across seeds (technique 8).** Li, Yosinski, Clune, Lipson, Hopcroft, "Convergent Learning: Do different neural networks learn the same representations?" ([arXiv 1511.07543](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07543)) use "a bipartite matching approach that makes one-to-one assignments between neurons". Run 3 uses the same idea with time-course probes and turns it into predictions tested on new models.
- **Faithfulness scores depend on the ablation (technique 9 caution).** Miller, Chughtai, Saunders, "Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics are not Robust" ([arXiv 2407.08734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08734)): "we find existing methods are highly sensitive to seemingly insignificant changes in the ablation methodology." Our caution is a different point: even a fixed ablation that keeps average accuracy can change a quarter of individual answers. A search did not find a source testing that directly; this is unchecked beyond that search.
- **Several training seeds (technique 11).** Sellam and 11 co-authors, "The MultiBERTs: BERT Reproductions for Robustness Analysis" ([arXiv 2106.16163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.16163)), "a set of 25 BERT-Base checkpoints", motivated by experiments that "are often based on a single checkpoint". Checking claims on independently trained models is established practice; ARC's own post names training seeds as the better test for the challenge.
